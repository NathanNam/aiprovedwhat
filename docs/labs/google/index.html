Claims

GO01 · Google · Announced November 25, 2026 Factoring in proven time L[1/3, 105]? A paper attributed to Google’s Cogentic agent (Gemini 4) claims a Las Vegas Turing machine that outputs the complete, AKS-certified prime factorization of every integer N in expected time L_N[1/3, 105] and memory L_N[1/3, 40], with no unproven hypotheses: the number field sieve’s exponent, proven, but with a constant far above the sieve’s 1.92, so no threat to RSA. Lean claimed, not released; no human check named. GO02 · Google · Announced November 25, 2026 Factoring with no coin flips, below exponential time? A 59-page paper from Google’s Cogentic agent (Gemini 4) claims deterministic factoring of every composite N in O(exp(3 (ln N)^(3/4))) bit operations, and deterministic quadratic nonresidues modulo any prime p in L_p[1/2] time, with no randomness and no unproven hypothesis. The README says a Lean 4 formalization exists; it has not been released. No human check is named. Not faster than randomized methods; no threat to RSA. GO03 · Google · Announced November 25, 2026 Discrete logarithms below quasi-polynomial time? A paper attributed to Google’s Cogentic agent (Gemini 4) claims a zero-error Las Vegas algorithm for discrete logarithms in finite fields of fixed characteristic in expected time exp((2 + o(1))(ln n)²/ln ln ln n) = (pn)^o(log n), with no unproven hypotheses: a divisor of ln ln ln n beyond Kleinjung and Wesolowski’s proven quasi-polynomial bound. As defined, its new method runs only for n of at least 2^(2^25088) (p = 2; our arithmetic), so nothing changes in practice. Lean claimed, not released; no human check named. GO04 · Google · Announced November 25, 2026 Past the square-root barrier for log-rank? A 37-page paper from Google’s Cogentic agent (Gemini 4) claims that every Boolean matrix of real rank r has a deterministic two-party protocol of C′ r^(1/2 − ε) bits, with ε = 5 × 10^−6 and C′ = 750 · 2^38: the first gain on the square root of the rank (Lovett 2014; Sudakov and Tomon 2023), if it holds. With these constants it beats √r only at ranks with millions of digits. The README says a Lean 4 formalization exists; it has not been released. No human check is named. GO05 · Google · Announced November 25, 2026 Rebuild a graph from its cards? A paper attributed to Google’s Cogentic agent (Gemini 4) claims Harary’s 1964 edge reconstruction conjecture for every finite simple graph with at least 1000 times as many edges as vertices (and every graph of minimum degree at least 2000), replacing Müller’s 1977 bound of about n log₂ n edges. By Müller’s bound, which the paper uses as its first step, it is new only for graphs with more than about 10³⁰¹ vertices (our arithmetic). Lean claimed, not released; no human check named. GO06 · Google · Announced November 25, 2026 1/5-approximate Nash equilibria in polynomial time? A 41-page paper from Google’s Cogentic agent (Gemini 4) claims a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm, MaximinKiteNE, that finds a 1/5-approximate Nash equilibrium (neither player can gain more than 1/5 by switching alone) in every two-player game with payoffs in [0, 1]: below the published 1/3 + δ (Deligkas, Fasoulakis and Markakis, 2022) and October 2026’s preprints, if it holds. Its fixed constants are astronomically large (an iteration cap with about 3.9 × 10^17 digits). The README says a Lean 4 formalization exists; it has not been released. No human check is named. GO07 · Google · Signed arXiv paper (Google, Illinois); proof found with Gemini 4 Carbon · posted to arXiv November 24, 2026 Hadwiger’s covering conjecture in 3D? Three named authors, Dror Aiger (Google), Sariel Har-Peled (University of Illinois) and Goran Žužić (Google Research), claim that every convex body in three dimensions is covered by eight smaller copies of itself, Hadwiger’s covering conjecture in dimension three (previous bound 14, Prymak 2023). Per the paper, an experimental Google harness on Gemini 4 Carbon discovered the proof “with minimal human steering”, an agentic harness produced a Lean 4 formalization (released in the arXiv files: 34 files, 13,725 lines), and the authors verified the mathematics. Not peer reviewed; not one of Google’s Cogentic papers.

Connections

Links to other labs’ results and to OpenAI’s catalogue: our own reading of the papers, not the labs’ data.

  • GO02 (Google) related by topic 142 (OpenAI’s catalogue). Both claim deterministic algorithms that avoid randomness and GRH: GO02 for integer factoring and quadratic nonresidues, 142 for factoring polynomials over prime fields (which would also give deterministic nonresidues by repeated square roots; our remark). Neither cites the other.
  • GO02 (Google) related by topic 003 (OpenAI’s catalogue). 003’s Corollary 1.2 claims a least quadratic nonresidue below C (log p)^A, which would far exceed GO02’s L[1/2] nonresidue bound; GO02 does not cite it (our reading). Both unreviewed.
  • GO04 (Google) related by topic 112 (OpenAI’s catalogue). Both claim to cross a square-root barrier in complexity theory, in different problems: GO04 the exponent 1/2 of the rank for deterministic communication (log-rank conjecture), 112 the 2^√n threshold for explicit depth-three circuit lower bounds. No OpenAI catalogue result concerns log-rank or communication complexity (our search).
  • GO06 (Google) related by topic 136 (OpenAI’s catalogue). Two sides of approximate equilibria: GO06 claims a polynomial-time algorithm for 1/5-approximate Nash equilibria in bimatrix games; 136 (OpenAI) claims a quasilinear PCP theorem for PPAD, whose corollaries are hardness results for approximate equilibria. No OpenAI catalogue result gives an approximate-Nash algorithm (our search).
  • GO07 (Google) related by topic 156 (OpenAI’s catalogue). Two classical covering problems of convex geometry: GO07 claims Hadwiger’s covering conjecture in three dimensions (eight smaller homothetic copies); 156 (OpenAI) claims a counterexample to Borsuk’s partition conjecture in dimension nine (pieces of smaller diameter). Neither paper cites the other.
  • GO07 (Google) related by topic 092 (OpenAI’s catalogue). Covering convex bodies by copies: GO07 by eight smaller homothets in three dimensions; 092 (OpenAI) the optimal order n log n of covering density by translates in high dimension. Neither paper cites the other.
