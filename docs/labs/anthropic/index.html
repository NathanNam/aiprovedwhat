AN01 · Anthropic · Announced September 4, 2026 Fermat’s Last Theorem, formalized in Lean Anthropic’s Lean proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem, written by Claude agents in 11 days: about 13 million lines, following Darmon, Diamond and Taylor. Anthropic makes Claude, which helps make this series. AN02 · Anthropic · Announced August 10, 2026 Two thirds of the zeta zeros, simple and on the line A result first found by Claude and written up by Levent Alpöge and Ralph Furman of Anthropic, proved again by Youness Lamzouri, with Lean by Anthropic and by Axiom. Anthropic makes Claude, which helps make this series. AN03 · Anthropic · Announced October 26, 2026 ETH false, a second time? A paper credited to Claude (Anthropic) claims a deterministic 2^o(n) algorithm for 3-SAT, refuting the Exponential Time Hypothesis, released the same day as OpenAI’s paper with the same claim (result 378). Purely asymptotic; Lean by Anthropic. Anthropic makes Claude, which helps make this series. AN04 · Anthropic · Announced, paper not public Fermat in Lean in 30 hours? Anthropic’s Fable 5.2 claim, no code yet Anthropic says about 1,000 Fable 5.2 agents redid its September Lean formalization of Fermat’s Last Theorem (AN01) in about 30 hours, with one-third the tokens and a proof seven times shorter. Its blog and system card say the agents started from 60 statements, definitions and 47 proved lemmas from the September run. No Lean code is public and no outside check is named. Known mathematics, not a new theorem. Anthropic makes Claude, which helps make this series. AN05 · Anthropic · Announced November 7, 2026 An exact limit for a quantum gravity bound? A Hexagon preprint contributed by Anthropic (author line “Claude (Anthropic)”, no named human author) claims the exact best slope Λ_V = 12π²/(π² + 4 arcsinh²1) ≈ 9.1266 of the spinless modular bootstrap bound Δ₁ ≤ c/Λ at large central charge, previously known only to lie between 8.503 and 12. Optimality proved by hand, attainment computer-assisted, finer details conjectured. Mathematical physics, at the edge of the series’ scope. Anthropic makes Claude, which helps make this series. AN06 · Anthropic · Announced; checked in Lean, no paper The Riemann hypothesis in Lean? An Anthropic mathematician’s proof with Claude, checked in Lean; no paper yet Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, says that working with Claude he found a proof of the Riemann hypothesis and of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions, formalized in Lean “without assumptions” (note, November 20, 2026). There is no paper, only a Lean archive of 175,187 files (about 17.4 million lines; our count). By our reading the main theorem is Mathlib’s own RiemannHypothesis and the companion statements are GRH for Dirichlet L-functions; not the Extended RH. On November 21 Andrew Sutherland (MIT) published a full build with only Lean’s three standard axioms, independent kernels accepted the proof, and the Lean and Mathlib communities said they have “full confidence” it is correct. No human-readable proof or peer review yet; not built or run by us. Anthropic makes Claude, which helps make this series. AN07 · Anthropic · Announced; checked in Lean, no paper The Riemann hypothesis in Lean, checked: what the kernels confirm, and what they don’t The follow-up to AN06. Levent Alpöge’s Lean proof of the Riemann hypothesis and of GRH for Dirichlet L-functions, made working with Claude, has been checked by people outside Anthropic. Andrew Sutherland (MIT; checks run with Claude Code) reports all 175,187 modules compiling with zero errors, only Lean’s three standard axioms, the exported proof accepted by the independent kernels nanoda and con-leche, and the statements matched exactly to Mathlib’s; the Lean and Mathlib communities say they have “full confidence” it is correct. That means the formal statements follow from the standard axioms according to those kernels, relying on Mathlib’s definitions. No paper, human-readable proof or peer review yet; not built or run by us. Anthropic makes Claude, which helps make this series. AN08 · Anthropic · Announced November 23, 2026 VP ≠ VNP over every field? A paper credited to Claude (Anthropic) claims that, over every field, arithmetic circuits for the Hamiltonian cycle polynomial, and for the permanent outside characteristic 2, need more than m^(c·(log m)^(1/3)) gates for all large m, so VP ≠ VNP: Valiant’s conjecture of 1979. Lean by Anthropic, passing its own CI; no human mathematician has checked the proof (README). Not P vs NP. Anthropic makes Claude, which helps make this series.

Connections

Links to other labs’ results and to OpenAI’s catalogue: our own reading of the papers, not the labs’ data.

  • AN03 (Anthropic) cites 378 (OpenAI’s catalogue). Both claim a deterministic algorithm that counts the satisfying assignments of a k-CNF in time 2^O(n log log n/√log n) for fixed k, refuting the Exponential Time Hypothesis; released the same day. Anthropic’s paper: “Independently and concurrently, an internal model at OpenAI claims to have proven a similar result [Ope26]” (paper p. 2, Provenance). OpenAI’s paper does not mention Anthropic.
  • AN06 (Anthropic) implies 003 (OpenAI’s catalogue). Our reading: the companion statement DirichletLNonvanishingRightOfHalf (Statements.lean, lines 6–8) gives L(χ, s) ≠ 0 for Re s > 1/2 for every Dirichlet character, which implies 003’s zero-free half-plane Re s > 7/8 for zeta and every Dirichlet L-function. The note also praises OpenAI’s “Siegel argument”, which we take to be 003’s Landau–Siegel paper (our inference). Independent checkers confirmed on November 21, 2026 that Lean accepts AN06’s proof with only the standard axioms (Sutherland’s log; Lean community statement); there is no paper or peer review yet.
  • AN08 (Anthropic) cites 108 (OpenAI’s catalogue). AN08’s paper cites OpenAI’s cubic lower bound for the border determinantal complexity of the permanent ([OAI26], paper p. 3). Its approximate theorem would imply, by its Remark N.6 (the one numbered statement not formalized in Lean), that the permanent is not approximated by determinants of polynomial size, far beyond a cubic bound, if it holds.
  • 379 (OpenAI’s catalogue) cites AN03 (Anthropic). The Euclidean paper says “The crossing construction originates in work of Anthropic” (Hexagon 2610.00577, Appendix B) and builds on its crossing decompositions; the Hamiltonian-cycle paper cites it, with OpenAI’s 378, as a precedent: “tensor-network algorithms for satisfiability”. (The November 23 paper adapted its grid method.) Anthropic makes Claude, which helps make this series.
