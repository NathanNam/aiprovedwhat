Axiom Math

Claims

AX01 · Axiom Math · Announced October 26, 2026 The classification of finite simple groups, in Lean Axiom Math’s Lean formalization of the classification of finite simple groups, about 43.1 million lines in 24 days, with a new written proof of the Unbalanced Group Theorem. AX02 · Axiom Math · Announced October 12, 2026 No zeros past 31/32, for every number field Dean Cureton and Jesse Thorner, with AxiomProver, claim a zero-free half-plane Re s > 31/32 for every Dedekind zeta function, building on OpenAI’s results 003 and 029. AX03 · Axiom Math · Announced September 17, 2026 Rogers–Ramanujan for every a, b Yifeng Huang, Kenny Lau and Ken Ono prove the Huang–Jiang–Oblomkov conjecture for every coprime pair 1 < a < b, with a Lean proof by Axiom. AX04 · Axiom Math · Announced October 14, 2026 Collatz: an explicit positive density (not a proof of Collatz) Kenny Lau, with AxiomProver, makes Lech Mazur’s positive-proportion theorem explicit, with the constant 2^2^2^163. Not a proof of the Collatz conjecture. AX05 · Axiom Math · Announced August 27, 2026 Faster random clocks, farther away? Only for p that is not even Colin Defant and Ken Ono (Axiom Math), with AxiomProver, prove that on every dihedral group raising symmetric clock rates never increases the ℓ^p distance to uniform when p is an even integer, and that every other p > 1 fails on some dihedral group. AX06 · Axiom Math · Announced November 4, 2026 The Stacks Project, all in Lean? Mostly AI-written, not yet human-reviewed Axiom Math’s Lean formalization of the Stacks Project, about 15.75 million lines: 16,219 of 16,249 formalizable tags counted as complete by its own data, about 97% of the formalized tags written by AI agents and not yet human-reviewed. Known mathematics; counterexamples to some printed statements are Axiom’s claims. AX07 · Axiom Math · Announced November 6, 2026 The Weil conjectures, all in Lean? AI-written, not yet human-reviewed Axiom Math’s Lean formalization of the four Weil conjectures, following Freitag–Kiehl (1988): about 980 modules that its README says were written by AI agents and not yet reviewed by humans. The decisive weight estimate comes from the unreviewed StacksFrontier tier of AX06. Its README says it proves verbatim an outside LeanEval statement (without the Betti numbers); our text check agrees the statement matches. Known mathematics, not a new theorem. AX08 · Axiom Math · Announced November 10, 2026 Cluster algebras, all in Lean? AI-written, with its assumptions stated Axiom Math’s Lean library for the first seven chapters of Fomin, Williams and Zelevinsky’s Introduction to Cluster Algebras: the Laurent phenomenon, Somos-4 and the finite type classification (with the E8 counts checked by Lean’s kernel) are in; positivity is not. Cited results and some exercises enter as stated hypotheses. Axiom’s project site says AI agents (Anthropic’s Claude) wrote it; the blog post does not mention AI. A comparator file for 11 headline statements and one CI build; no independent check yet. Known mathematics, not a new theorem. Anthropic makes Claude, which helps make this series. AX09 · Axiom Math · Announced November 12, 2026 Faltings’ theorem, all in Lean? AI-written, not yet reviewed by people Axiom Math’s Lean proof of Faltings’ theorem (curves of genus at least two over a number field have finitely many rational points), following Bombieri’s 1990 simplification of Vojta’s proof. The statement matches the LeanEval benchmark problem written by Junyan Xu (our text comparison). The README says about 430 modules were written by AI agents and not yet reviewed by humans; the code rests on Axiom’s AI-written StacksFrontier library (AX06). No CI or comparator run; partial outside builds. A known theorem, not new mathematics. AX10 · Axiom Math · Announced November 17, 2026 The minimal model program in Lean? OpenAI’s fourfold proof, AI-formalized, not yet reviewed Axiom Math’s Lean library for the minimal model program in dimension at most four. By Axiom’s account, its Lean theorems formalize the main theorem of OpenAI’s projective fourfold paper (result 056): every program for a projective log canonical fourfold with rational boundary, over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, has finitely many birational steps, and a maximal one ends at a nef adjoint or a Mori fibre space. By our reading the Lean statement matches the paper’s Theorem 1.2. The README says about 2,270 modules were written by AI agents and not yet reviewed by humans; the code rests on Axiom’s AI-written StacksFrontier library (AX06). No CI or comparator run. No new mathematics beyond result 056; the four Kähler papers are not covered.

Connections

Links to other labs’ results and to OpenAI’s catalogue: our own reading of the papers, not the labs’ data.

  • 018 (OpenAI’s catalogue) relies on AX01 (Axiom Math). Both 018 papers say they use the classification of finite simple groups: number fields, “The argument uses the classification of finite simple groups” (build/sections/finite-groups.tex, line 4); function fields, “The proof uses the classification of finite simple groups” (build/paper/A-finite-groups.tex, line 12).
  • 203 (OpenAI’s catalogue) relies on AX01 (Axiom Math). The Donovan paper over algebraic closures of prime fields says “We use the classification of finite simple groups” (build/sections/03_families.tex, line 903). Its Witt-vector companion does not mention it.
  • 206 (OpenAI’s catalogue) relies on AX01 (Axiom Math). Both 206 papers say “We use the classification of finite simple groups” (Finite congruence lattices, build/sections/affine.tex, line 29; Finite lattice representation problem, build/sections/introduction.tex, line 240).
  • 310 (OpenAI’s catalogue) relies on AX01 (Axiom Math). Only through cited reductions: the paper does not name CFSG, but builds on the component reductions of Aschbacher–Smith and Piterman–Smith (build/sections/01-introduction.tex, lines 59–62), which rest on the classification.
  • AX02 (Axiom Math) builds on 003 (OpenAI’s catalogue). AX02 cites both of OpenAI’s zero-free preprints: “OpenAI has released two proofs of nonvanishing in a fixed half-plane: Re s > 7/8 … and Re s > 11/12” (DedekindZeta.tex, line 104, Hexagon 2610.00200 v2 source). For the rational numbers its 31/32 is weaker than 003’s 7/8, so it is not an independent check of 003.
  • AX02 (Axiom Math) builds on 029 (OpenAI’s catalogue). AX02 follows 029’s strategy: “Following [Sections 4–8] of OpenAI’s primitive-roots preprint, we reduce nonvanishing of ζ_F to a bound for a smoothed reciprocal” (DedekindZeta.tex, line 191; also line 63).
  • AX10 (Axiom Math) builds on 056 (OpenAI’s catalogue). The README: “The four-dimensional theorem follows OpenAI, Termination for projective log canonical fourfolds with rational boundary (2026)” (README.md, line 5, at commit 1f9ade3); its main theorem MMP.Fourfold.Program.IsMaximal.isNefOnCurves_or_isMoriFibreSpace matches the paper’s Theorem 1.2 by our reading. The four Kähler papers of 056 are not covered.
