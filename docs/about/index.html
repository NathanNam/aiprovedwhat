About this project

AI systems have started posting proofs of research-level mathematics. Most people can’t read those papers, and most headlines don’t say whether anyone has checked them.

Each episode of AI Proved What? takes one claimed result, builds the intuition with animation, says exactly what was claimed and what wasn’t, and keeps a record of how far the proof has been checked. It’s for curious people without a math PhD: roughly, anyone who enjoyed high school math and wonders what the fuss is about.

How episodes are made

The scripts and animations are produced with AI assistance, using Anthropic’s Claude and the open-source Manim animation library. The narration is an AI-generated voice. Every episode links the paper it’s based on and publishes its full script, so anything said can be checked against the source.

The explanations simplify on purpose. A donut stands in for spaces with many more dimensions, and the on-screen pictures are cartoons of ideas, not exact diagrams.

How claims are tracked

Every episode carries the same four-step record: posted as a preprint; under expert review, meaning a named independent group has reviewed the proof (such as OSVerify) or is said to be checking it; peer reviewed; and checked in Lean, a computer program that verifies proofs step by step. Each record says when it was last checked. If a claim is withdrawn, the episode stays up and is marked in red.

When an episode is remade or replaced, the earlier videos stay listed under Versions of this episode on its page, unlisted on YouTube.

Results and papers

OpenAI groups the papers in its release into results: as of October 30, 2026, 721 papers in 373 results. A result is one main claim plus any companion papers, such as alternative proofs, supporting results or consequences, so one result can span several papers. Each episode explains one whole result, all of its papers included, and every result page lists those papers, with the first one OpenAI lists marked as the main paper.

Beyond OpenAI’s catalogue

The site also covers 14 results outside OpenAI’s catalogue: one-off OpenAI announcements (S01), Axiom Math (AX01–05), PSI (PSI01–05) and Anthropic (AN01–03). They get the same four-step record and their own episodes, and are kept out of the catalogue counts. See them all.

AI Proved What? is made with Claude, Anthropic’s AI. Anthropic’s own results (AN) are held to the same standards as every other lab’s, using only public sources, and their pages carry this disclosure at the top.

Corrections

If something in an episode is wrong, open an issue on GitHub or comment on YouTube. Fixes are noted on the episode page with the date.

Independence

AI Proved What? is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by OpenAI or any other organization whose work it covers. Names are used only to say where a claim comes from.
