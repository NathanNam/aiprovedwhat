Only the boundary counts?

Two AI preprints claim the two-dimensional area law for gapped quantum ground states

The claim, in plain words

A quantum state of many spins can be entangled in proportion to its size. Split a random state of 16 spins in half and the entanglement entropy is about 5, close to the maximum. Ground states of local, gapped Hamiltonians seem to be different: their entanglement grows only with the size of the boundary. That is the area law.

In one dimension, Hastings proved the area law in 2007. In two dimensions it became one of the main open problems of quantum information; a 2010 review asked whether gapped lattice models always obey it. The best results, by Anshu, Arad and Gosset, needed stronger assumptions, frustration-free models with a local gap, and even then held only for straight cuts, with a slowly growing extra factor.

Result 265 is two OpenAI preprints, 146 pages, dated September 24, 2026. The first, 90 pages, claims the area law on any finite induced region of the square lattice, holes allowed. For a unique ground state of a finite-range Hamiltonian with a spectral gap for the whole system, the entropy of any subset is at most a constant times its boundary, with no logarithm. No frustration-free or local-gap assumption is needed. The second, 56 pages, claims that on open L × L squares with nearest-neighbour terms, such ground states are approximated by projected entangled-pair states with polynomial bond dimension and error at most 1/L. That is an open-boundary version of a question posed on the torus.

What it doesn’t claim

Degenerate ground states, such as topological order on a torus; gapless systems; other lattices or three dimensions; any explicit constant or dependence on the gap; or an efficient algorithm.

How the proof goes

  1. Sub-volume first. The proof first shows a weaker, sub-volume law in boxes, then sharpens and upgrades it. The margins are extraordinarily thin: one key exponent is 1 − 10⁻⁵.
  2. Two families of tiles. A final step splits the region into two families of tiles so that the tile entropies cancel, leaving a strip along the boundary.
