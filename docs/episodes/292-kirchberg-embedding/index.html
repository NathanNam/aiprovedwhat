The claim, in plain words

The Cuntz algebra O₂ is Hilbert’s hotel turned into algebra: two isometries, each a perfect copy of the whole space, that together tile it, so one copy sends guest k to room 2k and the other to room 2k + 1. In matrices this is impossible: a trace would make 1 equal 2, and in any algebra with a trace the best attempt misses by at least 1/3. Kirchberg proved (announced in 1994, published with Phillips in 2000) that every separable exact C*-algebra sits inside O₂. He then asked whether every separable C*-algebra embeds in the norm ultrapower of O₂: whether O₂ can simulate any separable algebra to any finite accuracy, measured in the operator norm. The question, raised around 2007 and first in print in 2014, is a traceless cousin of Connes’ embedding problem. The 2020 preprint MIP* = RE answered Connes’ problem, but through traces, and O₂ has none.

Result 292 is one OpenAI paper, 14 pages, dated September 23, 2026, and published October 6. It claims that the full group C*-algebra of the explicit group ℤ[1/2]³ ⋊ (SL₃(ℤ) × ℤ), where (M, k) acts by v ↦ 2kMv, does not embed unitally in any norm ultrapower of any nonzero unital nuclear C*-algebra. Since O₂ is nuclear, Kirchberg’s question would have a negative answer.

What it doesn’t claim

Anything about trace-based ultrapowers, the reduced group C*-algebra, or targets that are not nuclear. The same setup appeared in August and September 2026 in work by Sauers and by Eckhardt, both made with AI, and the paper cites them; using it on Kirchberg’s question appears to be new.

How the proof goes

  1. A sharp test. The integer lattice twisted by 3×3 matrices has property (T): a spectral gap that makes “does this approximate representation contain that one?” a sharp yes or no.
  2. Finitely many matches. A new lemma says that in a nuclear algebra one approximate copy nearly matches only finitely many irreducible finite-dimensional representations.
  3. The zoom. The zoom that doubles every vector forces every match to come from another, so a finite list must repeat. A representation that sees parity would then come back after enough zooms, but after any zoom it no longer sees translations at all. So the embedding cannot exist.

0:00The question

The Cuntz algebra O₂ is Hilbert’s hotel turned into algebra: two isometries, each a perfect copy of the whole space, that together tile it, so one copy sends guest k to room 2k, the other to room 2k + 1. In matrices this is impossible: a normalized trace would make 1 equal 2, and a short trace argument shows that, in any algebra with a trace, the best attempt misses by at least 1/3; our computer finds matrices that miss by exactly that. Kirchberg proved, announced in 1994 and published with Phillips in 2000, that every separable exact C*-algebra sits inside O₂. Kirchberg then asked whether every separable C*-algebra embeds in the norm ultrapower of O₂: whether O₂ can simulate any separable algebra to any finite accuracy, measured in the operator norm. The question, raised around 2007 and first in print in 2014, is the traceless cousin of Connes’ embedding problem, which asks a similar question about a different algebra, with closeness measured by a trace. In 2020, the preprint MIP* = RE gave Connes’ embedding problem a widely accepted negative answer. But that argument runs through traces, and O₂ has none. Two authors wrote of Kirchberg’s question, in a paper revised as late as April 2026, that an entirely new approach appears to be necessary.

1:30The claim

Result 292 is one OpenAI paper, 14 pages, dated September 23 and published October 6, 2026. It claims that the full group C*-algebra of one explicit group does not embed unitally in any norm ultrapower of any nonzero unital nuclear C*-algebra. Since O₂ is nuclear, Kirchberg’s question would have a negative answer. The group combines vectors with dyadic coordinates, the integer 3×3 matrices of determinant one, and a zoom that doubles every vector. The same setup appeared in August and September in work by Sauers and by Eckhardt, both working with AI, and the paper cites them; using it on Kirchberg’s question appears to be new. Not claimed: anything about trace-based ultrapowers, the reduced group algebra, or targets that are not nuclear.

2:23How the proof goes

The core is property (T) of the integer lattice twisted by these matrices: a spectral gap that makes the question, does an approximate representation contain this one, a sharp yes or no. Our computer shows such gaps on finite quotients: for 3×3 matrices over the fields with 2, 3 and 5 elements they stay well away from zero, at 3.17, 2.71 and 1.77, while gaps for ever larger abelian groups fall below 0.01. A tuning-fork test reads 0 for a match and 6.3 otherwise; with a little noise, a match still reads only about 0.04. A new lemma says that in a nuclear algebra, one approximate copy nearly matches only finitely many irreducible finite-dimensional representations. The zoom forces every match to come from another match, so a finite list must repeat, and one representation that sees parity would come back after enough zooms. Our computer shows why it cannot: after even one zoom, and so after any number, it no longer sees the translations at all, and its overlap with the original is 0. So the embedding cannot exist.

3:38Where it stands

The paper is unrefereed and unchanged since release. In Lean, the full theorem is formalized, with the O₂ corollary, in about 18,500 lines; our scan finds no unproved steps, but we did not build it. Its property (T) proof rests on an integer certificate, a Gram matrix with 121 rows, and our computer recomputes its residual exactly, matching the Lean value to the last digit. OSVerify has not reviewed it, and we found no expert comment. O₂ contains every separable exact algebra. If this result holds, it cannot even approximately contain all separable algebras, and Kirchberg’s question has a negative answer.
