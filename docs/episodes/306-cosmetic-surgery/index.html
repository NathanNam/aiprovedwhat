Never the same twice?

The claim, in plain words

Dehn surgery on a knot in the 3-sphere removes a thickened neighbourhood of the knot and glues a solid torus back, so that a curve of slope p/q bounds the new disc. The first homology of the result is cyclic of order |p|, so slopes whose numerators differ in size always give different manifolds. The question is whether two different slopes on the same nontrivial knot can ever give the same manifold, with orientation. Gordon conjectured, for general manifolds with torus boundary, that they cannot; for knots in the 3-sphere this is the purely cosmetic surgery conjecture. With orientation reversed it does happen: on an amphichiral knot such as the figure-eight, slopes r and −r give mirror images, and on the right-handed trefoil slopes 9 and 9/2 do, found by Mathieu. Gordon and Luecke settled the meridian in 1989. Boyer and Lines, Ni and Wu, and Hanselman cut the possibilities down to slopes ±2 on a genus-two knot, or ±1/q; Daemi, Lidman and Miller Eismeier ruled out the second option; and Futer, Purcell and Schleimer checked more than 350 million prime knots, through 19 crossings.

Result 306 is one OpenAI paper, 144 pages, dated September 23, 2026, and made public October 6. It claims that distinct slopes on a nontrivial knot in the 3-sphere never give orientation-preservingly homeomorphic manifolds, meridian included, through Gordon and Luecke. The new part is the last case: slopes +2 and −2 on a genus-two knot. The reduction to that case is cited, not reproved, and rests partly on work by Daemi, Lidman and Miller Eismeier that is not yet published.

What it doesn’t claim

Chirally cosmetic surgery, knots in other 3-manifolds, links, or Gordon’s more general conjecture for manifolds with torus boundary.

How the proof goes

  1. A stack of cobordisms. The proof assumes an orientation-preserving homeomorphism between −2 and +2 surgery. It glues surgery cobordisms through that homeomorphism into a stack capped by symplectic pieces, and a count of instantons on the stack gives a nonzero number.
  2. Ends that must cancel. A moduli space of PU(2) monopoles on the same stack, cut down by phase conditions, is one-dimensional. Its ends are 2^η times that count, at the instantons, plus ends at lens spaces that cancel in pairs. The signed ends of a compact 1-manifold sum to zero, so the count would be zero: a contradiction. Most of the paper’s second half, about 60 pages, rules out every other kind of end.
