Same gas, two futures?

Two AI preprints claim nonuniqueness for the hard-sphere Boltzmann equation

The claim, in plain words

The Boltzmann equation describes a dilute gas: a density of particles in position and velocity that streams freely and changes through collisions. For hard spheres, each collision swaps the velocity components along the line joining their centres; our computer checks 3,000 exact collisions, and every one conserves momentum and energy. In a random-collision toy, two cold opposing beams relax toward a bell curve: their kurtosis goes from one to about three.

DiPerna and Lions proved in 1989 that any starting gas with finite mass, energy and entropy has a global solution, of a weak, renormalized kind. Whether such solutions are unique was not known: Silvestre wrote in 2022 that uniqueness within this class is currently unknown, and in August 2026 Gismondi, Golding and Novack conjectured that it fails, for a dense class of starting gases. Weak solutions were also not known to conserve momentum and energy locally.

Result 363 is two OpenAI papers. The September 23 paper, 65 pages, claims one starting density in a periodic box, with bounded velocities and finite mass, energy and entropy, that has two different global solutions, conserving mass locally and momentum in total, with energy only kept from growing. The October 5 paper, 78 pages, the headline, claims the same for a modified starting density, now with exact local conservation of mass, momentum and energy, and continuity in time for all time. Both were made public on October 6, 2026.

What it doesn’t claim

Not claimed: nonuniqueness for smooth data, or for a whole class of data; the starting density has unbounded peaks at smaller and smaller scales. It does not say whether the DiPerna–Lions solutions conserve locally; it says uniqueness fails even when local conservation is required. And it does not use convex integration, the method behind the 2026 conjecture. We found no competing claim.

How the proof goes

  1. Rings of jets at every scale. The starting gas is infinitely many shrinking rings of cold, opposing jets in fine stripes. At scale s the density is about 1/s but the mass only about s², so the totals stay finite: with all constants set to one, our computer finds the jets’ total mass is about 0.005 and their entropy about 0.02. One family of approximations stays dormant; the other gets a vanishing seed tuned to touch a ceiling.
  2. Branching beats the ceiling. Zooming in where the seed touches the ceiling gives a linear problem in a two-stream gas, where hot particles keep being produced. Their number grows by more than a factor of three per step, while the ceiling allows growth by at most (1 + a)⁴, which is less than three; our computer rechecks this arithmetic. So the zoomed-in hot gas is real and short-lived, while the dormant solution, zoomed the same way, shows nothing. So the two solutions must differ. The October paper adds a tilted jet axis and a background gas to get bounds for all time, and with them local conservation.
