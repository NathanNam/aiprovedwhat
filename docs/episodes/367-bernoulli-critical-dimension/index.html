Five, six or seven?

An AI preprint claims the critical dimension for one-phase Bernoulli minimizers is seven, closing dimensions five and six

Covers result 367 in OpenAI’s catalog: its one paper. See its connections to other results and the work it cites.

The claim, in plain words

Where does a positive quantity stop and zero begin? In the one-phase Bernoulli problem, studied by Alt and Caffarelli in 1981, a function is positive on part of a region and zero on the rest, and it minimizes its gradient energy plus the volume where it is positive. The edge of the positive part is the free boundary. In one dimension our computer finds the trade-off: a ramp up to height one over length l costs one over l plus l, smallest at slope exactly one, which is the free boundary rule: the gradient there equals one.

Zooming in at a free boundary point gives a cone, and a flat cone is just that ramp in one direction. The question is the first dimension with a minimizing cone that is not flat. Caffarelli, Jerison and Kenig ruled out dimension three in 2004, Jerison and Savin dimension four in 2015, and in 2009 De Silva and Jerison found a nonflat one in dimension seven. Our computer checks the symmetric cones: unstable through dimension six, stable from seven, matching published results. But that covers only symmetric shapes, and a paper in September 2026 still called dimensions five and six open.

Result 367 is one OpenAI paper, 50 pages, dated September 24 and made public October 6, 2026. It claims that in dimension six or below every nonzero one-homogeneous global minimizer is flat, so with the dimension-seven example, the critical dimension is seven. The consequence: free boundaries of minimizers are smooth through dimension six, have isolated singular points in seven, and above that a singular set of dimension at most n − 7.

What it doesn’t claim

Only dimensions five and six are new; the rest is cited. The claim is about minimizers, not every stable solution, and not the two-phase problem. And the new step rests on an exact computer calculation: the paper itself says the match between formulas and code, and the analytic argument, require mathematical review.

How the proof goes

  1. If five or six were the first bad dimension. Suppose five or six were the first bad dimension. Zooming in anywhere away from the tip drops to a lower dimension, where cones are flat, so the bad cone is smooth except at its tip, and its slice of the sphere is a smooth region. A five-dimensional cone gets a dummy sixth coordinate, so both cases live on the five-sphere, at the cost of two singular poles. Minimality then gives a stability inequality, with a sharp Hardy constant of four.
  2. An exact certificate. From the cone’s normalized Hessian the paper builds a test function and a vector field, and the key is a universal inequality on tensors in five dimensions. It is certified by two tables of rational coefficients, 231 rows and 557 rows, and 1,714 exactly bounded terms. Our computer re-adds the printed totals: an interior margin of about 0.037 and a boundary margin of about 0.22. Integrating over the slice then gives the interior margin times a positive integral, at most zero: a contradiction, so no such cone exists.
