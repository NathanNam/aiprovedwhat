The claim, in plain words

On a complex manifold X, the anticanonical bundle −KX is the top exterior power of the tangent bundle. When it is positive, as on a Fano manifold, its high powers have many sections: on the projective plane, 10, 28, 55 and 91 in degrees one to four. Suppose instead it only carries a smooth Hermitian metric of nonnegative curvature. That makes it nef, but it does not obviously give any section. In a positive-multiple form of a question Yau posed: must some power −mKX, m > 0, have a nonzero section?

The multiple matters. On an Enriques surface E the canonical bundle is nontrivial but its square is trivial, so it has no section. On E times the projective line, the anticanonical bundle restricts to the canonical bundle of E and to degree two on each line; a Ricci-flat metric on E and the round metric on the line make it semipositive. It has no section, but its square has five, and it is not torsion.

For anticanonical bundles that are only nef, Lazić, Matsumura, Peternell, Tsakanikas and Xie proved numerical effectivity for threefold pairs, and Müller proved actual nonvanishing in dimension three, and in every dimension when the bundle is semiample on the fibers of the maximal rationally connected quotient. A smooth semipositive metric need not give semiampleness: blow up the plane at nine suitable points on a smooth cubic, and the anticanonical bundle has such a metric, yet no power of it is generated by its sections (Koike explains why).

Result 068 is seven OpenAI preprints, all dated September 26, 2026, 270 pages in all. The headline paper (27 pages) claims the answer in every dimension: if −KX of a smooth connected complex projective variety has a smooth semipositive metric, then H0(X, −mKX) ≠ 0 for some m > 0. The six companions give alternative routes and further results: a bounded-metric criterion for klt pairs, used on torus quotients; removing a fixed error from twisted sections while keeping invariance; invariant Euler characteristics; integrable metrics and effectivity; metric descent along fibrations; and exact orders of vanishing. Five reuse the headline paper’s structure theorem, and the last adds a second descent through a fibration of Matsumura and Wang.

What they don’t claim

The metric must be smooth and semipositive. Anticanonical bundles that are only nef, with no such metric, are not covered, and the headline theorem gives no bound on the power m.

How the proof goes

  1. Splitting the cover. Yau’s theorem turns the semipositive curvature into the Ricci form of a Kähler metric with nonnegative Ricci curvature. The universal cover then splits as flat space, times a compact Ricci-flat factor F, times a compact factor Z that is rationally connected and has no holomorphic forms.
  2. The residual torus. The flat factor and F carry canonical frames, so the problem moves to Z. The deck transformations act on Z, possibly with infinite image; after a finite cover, the closure of that action is a compact torus. A torus-invariant anticanonical section on Z, multiplied by the frames, descends to a finite cover of X, and its norm, the product over the sheets, is a section on X.
  3. Finite volume. On Z the paper proves, by induction on dimension, that if J = −KZ + D has a semipositive metric of finite adjoint volume and L has a semipositive metric with bounded weights, then some aL + bD has a section. If not, a movable curve class α meets L and D trivially.
  4. A smaller base. With no holomorphic forms, χ(OZ) = 1; Riemann–Roch and hard Lefschetz give twisted forms whose determinants are sections of M + miL with mi → ∞. A slope bound makes M·α ≤ 0, so no system of these sections is generically finite, and a maximal one defines a fibration onto a smaller base.
  5. Moments. Fiber integrals move the problem to the base: the zeroth integral gives a finite-volume metric, and high moments, after taking roots, approach the fiberwise maximum and give a bounded metric. Induction produces a section that lifts with no forbidden poles.
  6. Invariance. An invariant version of the transfer on a torus quotient, where a dense orbit makes the invariant relative sections one-dimensional and averaging is an orthogonal projection, gives the torus-invariant section.

1:28The claim

Result 068 is seven OpenAI preprints, all dated September 26, about 270 pages in all. The headline paper, 27 pages, claims the answer in every dimension: if the anticanonical bundle of a smooth complex projective variety has a smooth semipositive metric, then some positive power of it has a nonzero section. The six companions give alternative routes and further results: a bounded-metric criterion for singular pairs, used on torus quotients; removing a fixed error from twisted sections while keeping invariance; invariant Euler characteristics; integrable metrics; metric descent along fibrations; and exact orders of vanishing. 2:22Splitting the universal cover

The proof starts from geometry. Yau’s theorem turns the semipositive curvature into the Ricci form of a Kähler metric, so the Ricci curvature is nonnegative. By the structure theorem of Demailly, Peternell and Schneider, refined by Campana, Demailly and Peternell, the universal cover splits as flat space, times a compact Ricci-flat factor F, times a compact factor Z that is rationally connected and has no holomorphic forms. The flat factor and F carry canonical frames, so the problem moves to Z. But the deck transformations also act on Z, and that action can be infinite. After a finite cover, its closure is a compact torus. So the paper needs an anticanonical section on Z fixed by the torus. Multiplied by the frames, it gives a section on a finite cover of X, and the norm, the product over the sheets, brings it down to X.

3:16Finite volume and induction

On Z the paper proves a more general statement, by induction on dimension. Take a line J, the anticanonical bundle plus an effective divisor D, with a semipositive metric of finite volume, and a line L with a bounded semipositive metric. Then some power of L has a section with poles only along D. If not, L plus D is pseudoeffective but not big, so a movable curve class α meets both trivially. Because Z has no holomorphic forms, its holomorphic Euler characteristic is one. Riemann–Roch and hard Lefschetz then give twisted differential forms, and their determinants are sections of one fixed line M plus growing multiples of L. The finite volume bounds cotangent slopes against α, so M has degree at most zero on α. No system of these sections can be generically finite, and a maximal one defines a fibration onto a smaller base S. Ratios of the sections give a rational section of a power of L along the fibers. Integrating its powers over the fibers moves the problem to S. The zeroth integral gives the base a finite-volume metric. High moments, after taking roots, approach the maximum on each fiber, and give a bounded metric. Induction on S produces a section, and it lifts back to Z with no forbidden poles. The torus-invariant section comes from an invariant version of the same transfer, onto a torus quotient. On the projective line, anticanonical sections are vector fields, and averaging over rotations keeps only the rotation field itself, which vanishes at the two poles. In general, a dense orbit makes the invariant relative sections one-dimensional in every degree, and averaging is an orthogonal projection, so Berndtsson’s positivity of direct images keeps that line semipositive.

5:08Where it stands

None of the seven preprints is peer reviewed, and none has been revised. There is no Lean formalization of the theorem; the release’s Lean library has only a few uncatalogued files covering side lemmas. The companions cite the headline paper, which cites none of them, and none cites papers from other results in the release. Inputs include Yau’s theorem, the structure theorems of Demailly, Peternell, Schneider and Campana, Bergman kernel positivity of Berndtsson and Păun, slope criteria for foliations, and hard Lefschetz with multiplier ideals. We found no public assessment. If it holds, then on every smooth projective variety, a smooth semipositive anticanonical bundle has a power with a nonzero section, in every dimension. Anticanonical bundles that are only nef, with no such metric, are not covered.
