Coins don’t help?

A claimed proof that L = RL = BPL: randomness adds nothing to logarithmic-space computation

Covers result 103 in OpenAI’s catalog: its one paper.

The claims, in plain words

A logarithmic-space machine reads its input freely but can write only about log n bits: room for a few counters and pointers into the input, not for a copy of it. L is the class of problems such machines decide deterministically; they automatically run in polynomial time. RL and BPL give the machine fresh random coins and require polynomial time: RL never accepts a no-instance and accepts a yes-instance with probability at least 1/2, and BPL errs with probability at most 1/3 either way. The classic example is undirected connectivity: Aleliunas, Karp, Lipton, Lovász and Rackoff showed that a polynomial-length random walk finds a connected target, and asked whether such computations can be done deterministically in logarithmic space.

Nisan’s pseudorandom generator replaced the coins with a seed of O(log² n) bits, and Nisan later simulated these machines deterministically in polynomial time and O(log² n) space. Saks and Zhou reduced the space to O(log3/2 n), without the polynomial time bound, and Hoza improved this by a factor of about √(log log n). Reingold’s 2005 algorithm put undirected connectivity in L, but general directed computations stayed open. Cheng and Hoza showed that a suitable hitting-set construction for read-once branching programs would give L = BPL.

Result 103 is one OpenAI preprint (108 pages) dated September 23, 2026. It claims L = RL = BPL. More precisely, for any randomized machine with polynomial running time and logarithmic space, a deterministic algorithm approximates its acceptance probability within 2−q, using O(log n + q) space and (n + 2)O(1)·2O(q) time; any fixed inverse-polynomial accuracy therefore costs only logarithmic space and polynomial time. The paper also gives an effective compiler that turns a bounded-error randomized program, with supplied time and space bounds, into a deterministic decider with explicit bounds.

What it doesn’t claim

The question for polynomial time, whether BPP = P, is separate and untouched. The randomized classes here require polynomial running time; randomized logarithmic-space machines without a time bound, studied by Borodin, Cook and Pippenger, are a different setting. The paper does not go through a hitting-set construction; it takes a different route, through a matrix inverse.

Last checked October 13, 2026. The preprint has not been revised. OpenAI published a Lean formalization, 39 files and 43,045 lines in an explicit Turing-machine model, matched against its comparator statement L = RL ∧ RL = BPL, with no unproved steps that we could find. The compiler and the explicit bounds are not part of that target. By our reading, the formalization follows a variant of the paper’s route: it proves its own mixing bound by an elementary Fourier argument instead of citing Shalom’s property (T) results, compares endpoints exactly instead of by fingerprints, uses fourth moments and ends with an exact average. We have not run the check. The paper is not among those OSVerify reviewed, and we have seen no expert review.

How the proof goes

  1. A matrix inverse. Stamp every configuration of the machine with the time, so every transition moves forward. The transition probabilities form a strictly forward substochastic matrix S, and the acceptance probabilities from every configuration are p = (I − S)−1e, where e marks the accepting configurations.
  2. Corrections and copies. A correction replaces a transition matrix C by D = C − E + EC. Then I − D = (I + E)(I − C), so the removed transitions move into a reward: (I − C)−1r = (I − D)−1(I + E)r. Copy steps split vertices to spread large entries. After l stages the error is at most |V0|·2−(H−1)l for a fixed H ≥ 4, so O(log n) stages give inverse-polynomial accuracy, with polynomially many vertices at each stage.
  3. One random environment. Each estimate uses one random environment of O(log n) bits, shared by all its recursive parts. Sampled tables with few entries per row stand in for the exact matrices; conditional averaging, with a mixing bound from Shalom’s results on property (T), reduces the noise; lists are compressed into exceptions to a default; and endpoints are compared by short fingerprints from polynomial evaluation and affine hashing. An eighth-moment bound shows that more than three quarters of the relevant environments give an accurate estimate.
  4. Logarithmic space. Each recursive call gets an allowance, and the data it keeps is bounded by multiples of the change in allowance and the change in the requested digit place, so the bounds telescope along any chain of calls. Digits are requested one at a time, a shared catalytic bit vector serves the fingerprint comparisons and is restored, and incoming edges are found by traversing program trees, with the contour idea of Cook and McKenzie. Every estimate halts in logarithmic space, accurate or not; enumerating the relevant environments, taking the median and comparing it with 1/2 decides the language deterministically.

The numbers in the video

  • A toy randomized machine, a weighted random walk on 6 vertices for 8 steps (54 time-stamped configurations): (I − S)−1e gives acceptance probability 0.0838 from the start; a simulation of 200,000 runs gives 0.0839.
  • The correction identity I − D = (I + E)(I − C), with the reward transfer, on 200 random examples: largest error about 9 × 10−16.
  • Space at n = 109 with constants set to 1: log n ≈ 30 bits, Hoza’s bound ≈ 74, log3/2 n ≈ 163, log² n ≈ 894.
  • Random walks of length 2|V||E| on random connected 60-vertex graphs: 400 of 400 found the target.
  • If more than three quarters of a list of estimates are within 1/8 of the truth, so is the median: 2,000 of 2,000 random trials with the bad estimates placed far away.

The preprint is not peer reviewed and has not been revised. The Lean formalization covers the class equality, by a variant of the paper’s route: it proves its own mixing bound instead of citing Shalom’s results, and compares endpoints exactly instead of by fingerprints.
