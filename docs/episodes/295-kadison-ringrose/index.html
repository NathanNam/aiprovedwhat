The claim, in plain words

A von Neumann algebra is an algebra of operators closed under adjoints and weak limits; the type II₁ factors among them carry a trace. Hochschild cohomology asks whether every cocycle, a multilinear map whose coboundary vanishes, is the coboundary of a bounded map. In degree 1 this says every derivation is inner, which Kadison and Sakai proved in 1966. Kadison and Ringrose built the theory in 1971, and the conjecture that all bounded cohomology of a von Neumann algebra with coefficients in itself vanishes bears their names; Sinclair and Smith trace it to 1967. It gave way case by case: type I and hyperfinite algebras by 1972, properly infinite and McDuff ones in 1987, algebras with Cartan subalgebras in 1998, and factors with property Γ in 2003. For the free group factors, Pop and Smith wrote in 2010, “nothing is known about their cohomology.”

Result 295 is one OpenAI paper, 28 pages, dated September 23, 2026, and published October 6. It claims that for every complex von Neumann algebra and every degree at least 2, every bounded cocycle with coefficients in the algebra is the coboundary of a bounded map with values in the algebra. Together with Kadison and Sakai’s theorem in degree 1, every positive degree would vanish: the Kadison–Ringrose conjecture.

What it doesn’t claim

Bounded here means ordinary norm bounded, with no complete boundedness assumed. Not claimed: other coefficient modules, C*-algebras, or one constant for all algebras, though for normal cocycles in the tracial type II₁ case the primitive is no larger than the cocycle. The paper does not use the release’s similarity result from episode 288, and we found no theorem that derives one from the other.

How the proof goes

  1. A walk instead of an average. There is no invariant average over the unitaries of a free group factor. The paper builds one random walk on the unitary group whose long products, at chosen lengths, have the moments of free Haar unitaries, and averages along it. That gives only a harmonic map, but a new Liouville theorem shows that a bounded harmonic map of this kind is a module map.
  2. A circle and a Fourier squeeze. The Liouville proof runs through a martingale and ultrapowers to a circular element, a scaled sum of free Haar unitaries. It ends with Fourier sums: sine sums never exceed 1.852, the Gibbs constant, while cosine sums grow like log m, squeezing the remaining difference to zero. That step fails without a trace.

The numbers in the video

  • Ours: for 2×2 matrices, coboundary ranks 3, 13, 51 and 205 leave no cohomology in degrees 1 to 3; the dual numbers, not an operator algebra, keep one dimension in each degree 1 to 4.
  • Ours: on 3×3 matrices, harmonic maps form a 9-dimensional space (the module maps) when the walk’s steps generate the algebra, and 27 for diagonal steps.
  • Ours: a random-matrix model of the circular element has norm 1.944 and moments 1.00, 1.97, 4.81, 13.11, approaching the Catalan numbers 1, 2, 5, 14.
