Every category finds a floor?

The claim, in plain words

Representations of a group can be added, multiplied by tensor product, and swapped: V ⊗ W is the same as W ⊗ V. A category with these features, finitely many simple pieces, enough projectives, and duals, is a finite symmetric tensor category. Is every such category secretly representations of a group, seen through a fiber functor: a faithful, exact translation into a simpler target that respects the tensor product and the swap? In characteristic zero, Deligne proved in 2002 that super vector spaces are a common target, for all categories of moderate growth.

In characteristic p, new targets are needed: the Verlinde categories. The first, Verp, keeps the Jordan blocks of a cyclic group of order p and throws away the largest one. Our computer checks the rules in characteristic five: two Jordan blocks of size three multiply to blocks of sizes one, three and five; drop the five, and the third simple object squares to the first plus itself. Its Frobenius–Perron dimension is the golden ratio. Benson, Etingof and Ostrik, and independently Coulembier, built a tower of higher Verlinde categories, Verpn, and Benson, Etingof and Ostrik conjectured that every category of moderate growth maps into this tower.

Result 208 is one OpenAI preprint, Fiber functors for finite symmetric tensor categories in positive characteristic (26 pages, dated September 24, 2026). It claims the finite case of that conjecture: for every prime p, including two, every finite symmetric tensor category over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p has a fiber functor to some level Verpn of the tower. The level depends on the category, and no bound on it is given.

What it doesn’t claim

Categories that are not finite (the full conjecture of moderate growth); a functor at a prescribed level; or uniqueness, except in characteristic two, where an earlier theorem already allows at most one. Along the way, the paper proves a conjecture of Coulembier and Flake about tilting modules for SL2, its main new ingredient.

How the proof goes

  1. Restricted tilting. The new theorem (Theorem 3.1): restrict the Steinberg module to a copy of an elementary abelian group of order pr inside SL2, tensor it with any representation, and the result is the restriction of a tilting module. The proof lifts the representation to a vector bundle on the plane minus the origin, extends it across the origin, and compares fibers. With this, Coulembier and Flake’s higher Frobenius functor becomes symmetric monoidal.
  2. A higher Frobenius functor. The paper takes pr-th tensor powers of objects and reads them through that functor into the category tensored with Verpr, choosing r and the embedding so that the functor is exact (Theorem 6.5).
  3. Squeezing. The Frobenius–Perron dimension never increases along a finite graph of simple objects (Section 7). Iterating finitely many times leaves only a part where earlier results of Etingof with Ostrik, and of Etingof with Gelaki, give a target. Everything lands in one level, Verpn.

The numbers in the video

  • Ver5 (ours): J3 ⊗ J3 = J1 + J3 + J5, so L3 ⊗ L3 = L1 + L3, and the Frobenius–Perron dimension of L3 is 1.618034, the golden ratio.
  • Further checks (ours, not on screen): Verp fusion rules for p = 2, 3, 5, 7 computed two ways (35 pairs agree); the paper’s Lemma 6.2 in 9,595 brute-force tests; Lemma 7.1’s identity in 8 cases. The Lean line count is ours.
