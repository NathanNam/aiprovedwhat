The claim, in plain words

At a prime p and a height n, the K(n)-local sphere is the part of the sphere spectrum that Morava K-theory sees. Its homotopy groups are modules over the p-adic integers. In their memoir, Hovey and Strickland asked whether each of them is finitely generated, noting that the evidence suggested it was true but subtle, and Hovey wrote on their own problem list that they believed we know almost nothing about these groups. Finitely generated, not finite: degrees 0 and −1 each contain a whole copy of the p-adic integers. Height 1 is essentially the image of J. Height 2 was known at primes 5 and up, from Hovey and Strickland’s analysis of Shimomura’s computations, and in 2024 Barthel, Schlank, Stapleton and Weinstein found the rational answer at every height: 2ⁿ rational dimensions spread over degrees 0 to −n². The torsion was the problem; experts expected the answer to follow from the chromatic splitting conjecture, whose strong form another paper in this release claims fails at height 3, for primes 5 and up.

Result 313 is one OpenAI paper, 103 pages, dated September 24, 2026, and made public October 6. It claims that every homotopy group of the K(n)-local sphere is finitely generated over the p-adic integers, at every prime, every positive height and every integer degree, and the same after K(n)-localizing any finite p-local spectrum. In other words, each group is finitely many copies of the p-adic integers plus a finite group, so the torsion in each degree is finite.

What it doesn’t claim

Bounds on the number of generators or the size of the torsion, new computations at height 2 or above, spectra that are not finite, or degrees other than integers. It does not assume the chromatic splitting conjecture.

How the proof goes

  1. Reduce to the stabilizer group. Three pages reduce the theorem to one statement: the continuous cohomology of the Morava stabilizer group, with coefficients in Morava E-theory mod p, is finite in each degree. Descent turns that into finite homotopy mod p, and a compact form of Nakayama’s lemma then lifts finiteness mod p to finite generation.
  2. Eighty-eight pages for the hard part. The coefficient theorem takes about 88 pages of p-adic geometry and group cohomology: the Fargues–Fontaine curve, Banach–Colmez spaces, results of Anschütz and Le Bras, Kummer theory and Cartier transfers, ending with a Baire category argument that a countable compact Hausdorff group must be finite.

2:40How the proof goes

Three pages reduce the theorem to one statement: the continuous cohomology of the Morava stabilizer group, with coefficients in Morava E-theory mod p, is finite in each degree. Descent turns that into finite homotopy mod p, and a compact form of Nakayama’s lemma then lifts finiteness mod p to finite generation. Our computer illustrates the stabilizer group: its standard open subgroups have index (pⁿ − 1)·p^(n(k − 1)), and as a p-adic Lie group it has dimension n². The coefficient theorem takes about 88 pages of p-adic geometry and group cohomology: the Fargues–Fontaine curve, Banach–Colmez spaces, results of Anschütz and Le Bras, Kummer theory and Cartier transfers, ending with a Baire category argument that a countable compact Hausdorff group must be finite. At height 1, our computer runs the descent by hand: degree zero grows like 3ᵃ as we work mod 3ᵃ, while each torsion degree freezes, which is exactly what finite generation looks like.

If it holds, every homotopy group of the K(n)-local sphere is a finitely generated module over the p-adic integers, at every height and every prime: the torsion in each degree is finite.
