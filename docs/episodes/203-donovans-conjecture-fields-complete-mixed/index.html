The claim, in plain words

The group algebra of a finite group over a field of characteristic p breaks into blocks, and each block has a defect group: a subgroup of prime-power order that measures how far the block is from being as simple as a matrix algebra. Two blocks count as the same if their categories of modules are equivalent, called Morita equivalence; it ignores matrix sizes. One block of the alternating group on five letters at the prime two has dimension 16, yet it is Morita equivalent to the field itself.

Donovan’s conjecture, recorded by Alperin around 1980: fix the defect group, and however large the group, only finitely many blocks occur up to Morita equivalence. In our table of 69 blocks, all 10 whose defect group has order two share the same invariants, and blocks with the Klein four-group as defect group come in exactly three kinds, as is known. As the papers tell it, the conjecture was known for some defect groups, such as abelian 2-groups, and in August 2026 a human team finished the tame case. Earlier, Kessar had split the problem in two: bound the size of the blocks, measured by their Cartan matrices, and bound the field they can be written over.

Result 203 bundles two OpenAI preprints. Donovan’s Conjecture over Algebraically Closed Fields (94 pages, dated September 24, 2026), Theorem 1.1: fix a prime p, an algebraically closed field K of characteristic p and a bound M; blocks of all finite groups with defect groups of order at most M fall into finitely many K-linear Morita classes, for every prime, including 2. Integral Donovan Finiteness over Witt Vectors (22 pages, dated September 25, 2026), which builds on the first, claims the stronger integral version: over the Witt vectors W(F̄p), and over every complete discrete valuation ring of characteristic zero whose residue field is algebraically closed.

What it doesn’t claim

An explicit list of Morita classes, or any bound on its length; Puig’s conjecture, the stronger source-algebra form; fields that are not algebraically closed. And unlike result 202, this proof uses the classification of finite simple groups (field paper, p. 33).

How the proof goes

  1. Size. Using the classification, the field paper bounds the Cartan numbers of every block of every quasisimple group with small defect group (Theorem 4.1). Sporadic groups are only finitely many, and alternating groups follow from earlier work. The hard case is groups of Lie type in cross characteristic, which can grow without bound while the defect group stays small: for bounded rank, geometry on flag varieties; for unbounded rank, moving beads between the runners of an abacus; and a special endpoint the paper calls the cuspidal triangle. This part runs to almost 60 pages.
  2. Field. Up to Morita equivalence, any block is a crossed extension of a base block built from quasisimple pieces. The paper compares each base block with its twist by a bounded power of Frobenius, in a way that respects the action of a Sylow subgroup (Proposition 9.1); the leftover scalar error is removed because p-groups have no positive-degree cohomology with these coefficients. With both the size and the field bounded, only finitely many Morita classes remain (Theorem 8.8).
  3. Integral version. The second paper upgrades the comparison over the Witt vectors, where the errors are Teichmüller units, and removes a large kernel of order prime to p.

The numbers in the video

  • Blocks (ours), computed directly in the group algebra over a splitting field: 33 pairs of small groups and primes, 69 blocks; in every block the largest elementary divisor of the Cartan matrix equals the order of the defect group.
  • Klein four-group at p = 2 (ours): three fingerprints, the Klein four-group itself (Cartan matrix (4)), A4 and A4 × C3 (three simples, Loewy length 3), the principal block of A5 (three simples, Loewy length 5). Defect group of order two: one fingerprint across 10 blocks. A5 at p = 2: a block of dimension 16 with basic dimension 1.
  • The papers’ central-transfer lemma (Lemma 2.1) checked on 4 pairs (ours). The Lean line count is ours.
