The claim, in plain words

A trace measures the size of positive elements. Here traces are defined on positive elements, lower semicontinuous, with values up to infinity, and the cone of all of them, with a suitable topology, is the trace cone. For the functions on three points, and for the sum of matrix blocks of sizes 1, 2 and 5, the cone is the same cube [0, ∞]³, and its 8 elements with τ + τ = τ, traces taking only 0 and ∞, match the 8 ideals exactly. The Razak–Jacelon algebra W is simple and nuclear, with exactly one trace up to scaling, no nonzero projections even in matrices over it, and no K-theory. Tensoring with W and the compact operators throws away everything except, it was hoped, the trace cone. That is Robert’s question, recorded by Santiago in 2012 and listed by Schafhauser, Tikuisis and White as Problem LXVIII; the simple and traceless cases were known, but even the case of one proper nonzero ideal was still open.

Result 301 is one OpenAI paper, 62 pages, dated September 25, 2026, and published October 6. It claims that if two separable nuclear C*-algebras have isomorphic trace cones, as topological cones, then their tensor products with W and the compacts are isomorphic, for any ideal structure, even with both finite and infinite subquotients, and with no UCT assumption.

What it doesn’t claim

That the algebras themselves are isomorphic, or anything about tensoring with W without the compacts; anything beyond separable nuclear algebras; or a description of which cones can occur.

How the proof goes

  1. From the cone to an isomorphism. The proof turns the cone into the Cuntz semigroup of the stabilization, by Robert’s realification, then runs existence and uniqueness inside a sequence algebra, measuring errors ideal by ideal, and borrows Gabe’s ideal-related absorption.
  2. How the cone remembers ideals. Scaling a trace toward 0 or ∞ lands on the traces of its finite and zero ideals. For the unitized compacts a whole half-line of traces has the compacts as its finite ideal; for the unitized stable O₂ only the single ideal trace does, so the cones differ.

2:14How the proof goes

The proof turns the cone into the Cuntz semigroup of the stabilization, by Robert’s realification, then runs existence and uniqueness inside a sequence algebra, measuring errors ideal by ideal, and borrows Gabe’s ideal-related absorption. The topology on the cone uses cuts. Our computer shows why: the traces n times the point mass at 1/n give the value 1 to the function t for every n, while their limit gives 0, a gap that only the cut test handles. In a one-ideal example the cone sees the difference. For the unitized compacts, a whole half-line of traces has the compacts as its finite ideal, while for the unitized stable O₂, only the single ideal trace does. Scaling a trace toward zero or infinity lands on the traces of its finite and zero ideals, which is how the cone remembers where each trace is finite and where it vanishes.

3:11Where it stands

The paper is unrefereed and unchanged since release. In Lean, only a supporting step is formalized, in about 1,100 lines: the trace cone determines the ideal lattice, and each trace’s finite and zero ideals. The classification theorem itself is not formalized. Our scan finds no unproved steps, but we did not build it, and we found no expert comment. If it holds, a separable nuclear C*-algebra, once tensored with W and the compacts, is determined up to isomorphism by its cone of traces, whatever its ideals.
