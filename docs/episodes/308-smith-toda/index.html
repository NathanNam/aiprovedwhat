The claims, in plain words

In stable homotopy theory, Brown–Peterson homology at a prime p has coefficients a polynomial ring in classes v₁, v₂, …, with vₙ in degree 2(pⁿ − 1), and with v₀ standing for p itself. A Smith–Toda complex V(n) is a finite spectrum whose Brown–Peterson homology is that ring with p and v₁ through vₙ killed, built from 2ⁿ⁺¹ cells. V(0) is the mod-p Moore spectrum. V(1) for p ≥ 3, V(2) for p ≥ 5 and V(3) for p ≥ 7 were built by Adams, Smith and Toda by 1971, and nothing new was built for 55 years. Toda, Ravenel and Nave proved that V(n) cannot exist when p is small compared with n, so there is no V(4) at 7 or below, and Ravenel showed that the group that could receive the first differential on v₄ is nonzero at every odd prime, writing that the classical approach could be pushed no further. Hopkins and Smith’s periodicity methods give finite complexes that kill suitable powers of these classes, but not the classes themselves.

Result 308 is two OpenAI papers, 98 pages, both dated September 23, 2026, and made public October 6. The first claims that for every n there is some prime, not named and with no bound given, with a finite spectrum whose Brown–Peterson homology is the quotient by p and v₁ through vₙ, with its canonical comodule structure: Smith–Toda complexes at every height, when the prime may vary. The second, by an independent method, claims that V(4) exists at the prime 1009.

What they don’t claim

One prime for all heights, a bound on the prime, all large primes, or any multiplication on the new complexes. V(4) at 11 and the other primes below 1009 stays open. On the choice of 1009, the paper says only that it makes the finite inequalities in its calculation generous; it is the first prime after 1000.

How the proof goes

  1. All primes at once. The varying-primes paper works in an ultraproduct over all odd primes, where a p-fold power operation, made exact by a single Euler class, behaves uniformly in p. It then pulls a finite diagram back to one prime.
  2. Just in time, at 1009. The second paper rebuilds V(0) through V(3) with unital products, then shows that v₄ survives the Adams–Novikov spectral sequence. Only four possible obstructions remain; the first, a differential of length 2,017, is the one Ravenel’s warning was about, and the paper shows it is zero by an algebraic argument (it does not cite that passage; the link is ours). The last is killed by a relation involving β^((p − 1)²): the product it kills sits at filtration 2,032,133, just before the would-be differential of length 2,034,145.

The numbers in the video

  • Ours: at the prime 5, through degree 6,248, the quotient rings shrink from 841,298 dimensions to 3,857, then 82, 7 and 2 as p, v₁, … are killed (algebra only).
  • Ours: at 1009, v₄ sits in degree 2,072,977,845,120, and (p − 1)² = 1,016,064.
