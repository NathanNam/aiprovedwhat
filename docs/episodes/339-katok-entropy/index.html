Most chaotic only if symmetric?

An AI preprint claims Katok’s entropy rigidity conjecture in every dimension from three up

The claim, in plain words

On a negatively curved space, geodesics fan out exponentially, so the geodesic flow is chaotic. Two numbers measure how chaotic. Topological entropy counts the growth of distinct long orbits; Liouville entropy measures the chaos seen from a starting point and direction chosen uniformly at random. The second can never exceed the first. On a genus-2 surface of constant curvature minus one, both equal 1, and our computer counts orbit points growing at that rate. Bend the surface so its curvature varies, and our computer finds the Liouville entropy is about 0.96, below Katok’s bound of 0.975, which the topological entropy can never fall below.

In 1982 Katok proved that on surfaces the two entropies agree only in constant curvature, and wrote that it looks like a reasonable conjecture that locally symmetric spaces are the only negatively curved cases where the Liouville measure has maximal entropy. In higher dimensions these include complex, quaternionic and Cayley spaces, whose curvature is not constant; at a standard scale our computer reproduces Katok’s table of entropies, from 2 up to 22. Besson, Courtois and Gallot proved a related but different conjecture in 1995. Before this release, the main higher-dimensional result was Humbert’s: rigidity near real and complex hyperbolic metrics. A 1990 preprint by Hamenstädt claimed the three-dimensional case, but was never published.

Result 339 is one OpenAI paper, 66 pages, dated September 23 and made public October 6, 2026. It claims Katok’s conjecture in every dimension from 3 up: on a closed manifold with strictly negative curvature, the Liouville entropy equals the topological entropy exactly when the metric is locally symmetric, that is, when its universal cover is a rank-one symmetric space at some scale.

What it doesn’t claim

Not claimed: surfaces, which are Katok’s own theorem; manifolds whose curvature is only nonpositive; or a machine-checked proof, since there is no Lean.

How the proof goes

  1. From equal entropies to formal fields. Equal entropies mean that the expansion rate equals a constant up to a coboundary, by thermodynamic formalism and Livšic’s theorem; Katok suggested this route in 1985, and our symbolic toy shows the same equality case. The paper then records higher-order jets of the stable and unstable leaves, puts them in exact polynomial normal form, and builds two formal affine fields of covectors.
  2. A dichotomy, then classical rigidity. From these it builds a formal algebra of contact symmetries and proves a dichotomy: a finite-dimensional algebra yields formal stable and unstable fields, while an infinite-dimensional one yields special stable and unstable directions. The infinite case is ruled out with Busemann functions at infinity. In the finite case the stable and unstable splitting is smooth, and classical rigidity theorems of Benoist, Foulon and Labourie, and of Besson, Courtois and Gallot, then give local symmetry.

The numbers in the video

  • Ours: on a genus-2 surface of constant curvature −1 both entropies are 1; bent to area 13.21, its Liouville entropy is about 0.961, below Katok’s bound 0.975.
  • Ours: at a standard scale the rank-one symmetric spaces have entropies 2, 4, 10 and 22 (real 3-dim, complex 4-dim, quaternionic 8-dim, Cayley 16-dim), Katok’s table.
  • Ours: orbit points of a genus-2 surface group within distance 9: 2,057, against 2,025 predicted.
  • Ours: in a symbolic toy, Liouville entropy touches topological entropy (log 2) only when the two expansion rates are equal.
