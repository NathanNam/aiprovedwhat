The claim, in plain words

A Fano manifold is a smooth projective variety whose anticanonical bundle is ample, so in a sense it is positively curved, like projective space. Two numbers measure it. The Picard number ρ counts independent divisor classes. The pseudoindex ι is the smallest anticanonical degree of a rational curve on it. Projective space of dimension n has ρ = 1 and ι = n + 1; a product of two projective lines has ρ = 2 and ι = 2; blowing up the plane at a point creates a curve of anticanonical degree one, so ι = 1.

Mukai’s conjecture (1988) used the Fano index: the Picard number times the index minus one is at most the dimension. Bonavero, Casagrande, Debarre and Druel replaced the index by Wiśniewski’s pseudoindex, which is at least as large, making the statement stronger, and proved it up to dimension four; Andreatta, Chierici and Occhetta reached dimension five. It was also known for toric, horospherical and spherical varieties, and when the pseudoindex is more than a third of the dimension (Novelli, extending joint work with Occhetta).

Result 063 is one OpenAI preprint (September 24, 2026; 19 pages). It claims the generalized Mukai conjecture in every dimension: for every Fano manifold of positive dimension n, ρ(ι − 1) ≤ n, and equality holds exactly for a product of ρ copies of projective space of dimension ι − 1. For products of projective spaces the bound is easy to see: the pseudoindex comes from a line in the smallest factor, so equality needs all the factors to have the same size. As a special case it recovers Wiśniewski’s theorem that 2ι > n + 2 forces ρ = 1.

What it doesn’t claim

The statement is about smooth Fano manifolds. When ι = 1 the inequality is automatic, so the content is for manifolds all of whose rational curves have anticanonical degree at least two. The proof does not assume semisimple quantum cohomology, and it uses no extra symmetry.

How the proof goes

  1. Quantum matrices. For a basis D1, …, Dρ of divisor classes, quantum multiplication by Dj is a matrix Aj(q) on cohomology: cup product plus two-point corrections Sβ counting rational curves of degree β meeting two cycles. The matrices commute. A correspondence of anticanonical degree d lowers codimension by d − 1, so a nonzero product of ρ correspondences in independent curve degrees gives ρ(ι − 1) ≤ n. On ℙ1 × ℙ1, one ruling takes a point to a line and the other takes the line to the whole surface: a drop of 2 = n.
  2. Point descendants. Virtual counts of rational curves through a point, cut down by a power of a cotangent class, are bounded below by (ad)−(d−1) for curve degrees carried by free rational curves (a is fixed so that −aK is very ample). They satisfy a recurrence driven by the quantum matrices.
  3. Independent eigenvalues. If every joint eigenvalue tuple of the Aj satisfied a polynomial relation, the recurrence would force, after factorial normalization, decay faster than any exponential along a suitable free degree, while the lower bound allows only exponential decay. So one joint eigenvalue tuple is algebraically independent. On ℙ1 × ℙ1 the tuples are (±√q1, ±√q2).
  4. From eigenvalues to curves. An alternating trace of matrix-valued differentials, unchanged by any change of basis, detects the independent eigenvalues in a triangular basis and a nonzero product of independent curve correspondences in the fixed basis. That proves the inequality.
  5. Equality. At equality every chosen degree is minimal; the curves form proper families, the ordered-chain bound applies, and Occhetta’s characterization gives a product of projective spaces.

1:16The claim

Result 063 is one OpenAI preprint, 19 pages, dated September 24. It claims the generalized Mukai conjecture in every dimension: for every Fano manifold of positive dimension n, ρ(ι − 1) is at most n, and equality holds exactly for a product of ρ copies of projective space of dimension ι − 1. Earlier general approaches built chains of rational curves directly. This one starts from quantum cohomology, which counts rational curves meeting prescribed cycles, and brings in proper families of curves only at the very end, in the equality case.

2:22Quantum cohomology

Here is the idea. Choose a basis of divisor classes. Quantum multiplication by each one is a matrix acting on the cohomology of the manifold: the ordinary cup product, plus corrections that count rational curves of each degree meeting two given cycles, weighted by formal variables q. These matrices commute. A curve correspondence of anticanonical degree d lowers the codimension of a class by d minus one. So if a product of ρ correspondences in independent curve degrees is nonzero, the total drop, at least ρ(ι − 1), cannot exceed n. That is the inequality. On the product of two projective lines, each ruling has degree two. One correspondence takes a point to a line, the other takes the line to the whole surface: a total drop of two, equal to the dimension. The hard part is producing such a product in general, without assuming that quantum cohomology is semisimple. The paper turns to point descendants: virtual counts of rational curves through a point, cut down by a power of a cotangent class. It proves that for curve degrees carried by free rational curves, these numbers are bounded below by one over a d, to the power d minus one, for a fixed a.

3:42Eigenvalues and curves

The descendants obey a recurrence driven by the quantum matrices. Suppose every joint eigenvalue tuple of those matrices satisfied a polynomial relation. Then, after normalizing by factorials, the recurrence would force the descendants to decay faster than any exponential along a suitable free curve degree, while the lower bound allows only exponential decay. So one tuple of eigenvalues is algebraically independent. On the product of two lines, the eigenvalues are plus or minus the square root of q one, and plus or minus the square root of q two. An alternating trace of matrix-valued differentials, unchanged by any change of basis, turns that independence into a nonzero product of correspondences in independent curve degrees, which proves the inequality. At equality, every chosen degree is minimal. The curves then form proper families, the ordered-chain bound of Bonavero, Casagrande, Debarre and Druel applies, and Occhetta’s characterization shows that the manifold is a product of projective spaces.

4:52Where it stands

The preprint is not yet peer reviewed, and it has not been revised. There is no Lean formalization, and it cites no other paper from the release. Among its inputs are established tools: genus-zero Gromov–Witten theory with virtual classes, the divisor, string and recursion identities, the ordered-chain bound with its erratum, and Occhetta’s product theorem. We found no public assessment. If it holds, a Fano manifold whose rational curves all have high degree must have a small Picard number: when ι is at least two, ρ is at most n divided by the quantity ι − 1, and only products of equal projective spaces reach that bound.
