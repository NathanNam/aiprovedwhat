The claim, in plain words

A logarithmic Sobolev inequality says that a function cannot carry much entropy without paying in gradient energy: the entropy of f² is at most a constant times the average of the squared gradient. For the standard Gaussian, Gross’s inequality holds with the same constant in every dimension. It is stronger than a Poincaré inequality, which bounds only the variance, and it forces Gaussian concentration for every Lipschitz function, so in particular every linear function must have Gaussian tails. The two-sided exponential law has Poincaré constant 4 but no log-Sobolev inequality at all.

For log-concave measures, such as the uniform measure on a convex body, positive curvature would give a log-Sobolev inequality by the Bakry–Émery criterion, but log-concavity alone gives no positive curvature. Bizeul conjectured in 2023 that, for centered log-concave measures, uniformly subgaussian linear marginals with parameter a are enough for a log-Sobolev constant at most a universal multiple of a², in every dimension. The general bounds known before grew like n a² (from Bobkov’s criterion) and √n a² (Bizeul), and Bizeul proved the rotationally invariant case.

Result 093 is one OpenAI preprint dated September 23, 2026 (65 pages). It claims a universal constant C such that, in every dimension, every centered log-concave probability measure with a density whose linear marginals are a-subgaussian satisfies Ent(f²) ≤ C a² ∫|∇f|². Uniform measures on convex bodies are included, and no smoothness or positive curvature is assumed. Through a theorem of Otto and Villani the same constant gives a quadratic transport inequality.

What it doesn’t claim

The constant is not computed: the proof works by contradiction and gives no value. The theorem concerns centered log-concave laws with a density; the hypothesis controls only linear functions, and the conclusion then controls every smooth function.

How the proof goes

  1. A contradiction. If the theorem failed, truncation, a weak quadratic tilt and rescaling would give compactly supported log-concave laws with log-Sobolev constant normalized to one while their linear subgaussian parameters tend to zero. Gaussian noise of fixed variance, one eighth, is added to each law. An extremal function for its entropy inequality produces a second law and a vector field w, nearly a first eigenfunction of the associated diffusion; if the log-Sobolev and Poincaré constants coincide, the gradient of a spectral-gap eigenfunction plays this role.
  2. Prediction, then tensors. Observing a point through Gaussian noise of variance r, the best prediction of w keeps, in the limit, a fraction e−r of its mean square, on a fixed interval of noise levels. The tools are a formula of Guo, Shamai and Verdú for Gaussian channels and an entropy-loss comparison built from repeated Gaussian observations of independent copies. Gaussian polynomial orthogonality turns this exact curve into a hierarchy of symmetric tensor fields, each nearly the derivative of the one before, all of nearly the same size.
  3. Matrices and information. From the tensors comes a positive matrix field whose square root nearly attains equality in a Hilbert–Schmidt Poincaré inequality. Symmetry in the tensor indices forces its pieces to nearly commute, and a Hermite expansion along the diffusion, run for a time t, makes covariance fluctuations follow the lognormal law exp(√(8t) Z − 4t). Observing a Gaussian vector whose covariance depends on the matrix field, the log-Sobolev inequality bounds the mutual information, integrated over scales, by a universal amount, while the fluctuations push it up like log t. Large t gives the contradiction.

The numbers in the video

  • Linear subgaussian parameter (sup over unit θ of E exp(⟨X, θ⟩²/a²) ≤ 2) for variance-one laws: Gaussian a = 1.633 (a² = 8/3 exactly), uniform 1.338, two-sided exponential none.
  • The two-sided exponential density e−|x|/2: Poincaré constant 4 (our eigenvalue computation: 3.99 on [−120, 120]); with f = es|x|, Ent(f²)/∫f′² = 6.5, 14.9, 33.1, 188 and 1,979 at s = 0.3, 0.4, 0.45, 0.49 and 0.499.
  • The cube [−1, 1]n: a = 0.773 along a coordinate and never above √(8/9) ≈ 0.943 in any direction; by tensorization its log-Sobolev constant equals that of one interval in every dimension, while bounds of order n a² and √n a² grow with n.

The two-sided exponential law shows the difference. Its Poincaré constant is 4, but it has no log-Sobolev inequality at all: with test functions e^(s|x|), the ratio of entropy to energy in our computation passes 1,900 at s = 0.499, and keeps growing. Log-concave measures, like the uniform measure on a convex body, are where the question gets interesting. With positive curvature, the Bakry–Émery criterion gives a log-Sobolev inequality, but log-concavity alone gives no positive curvature. Since the inequality forces subgaussian linear functions, the natural question is the converse. For a centered log-concave measure whose linear marginals are uniformly subgaussian, with parameter a, is the log-Sobolev constant at most a universal constant times a², in every dimension? Bizeul formulated this conjecture in 2023. The general bounds grew with dimension: Bobkov’s criterion gives order n a², and Bizeul improved it to order √n a², and proved the rotationally invariant case. Some numbers. For a Gaussian of variance 1, the parameter a is about 1.63, a² exactly 8/3; for the uniform law with variance 1, about 1.34; for the two-sided exponential law, no finite a works. For the cube [−1, 1]ⁿ in n dimensions, a stays bounded: about 0.77 along a coordinate, and never above about 0.943 in any direction. Its log-Sobolev constant is that of a single interval in every dimension, because the cube is a product. For it, the old general bounds would grow like n and like √n. The conjecture asks for a dimension-free bound for every such measure, products or not.

2:32The claim

It claims a universal constant C such that, in every dimension, every centered log-concave probability measure with a density, whose linear marginals are a-subgaussian, satisfies the log-Sobolev inequality with constant C a². Uniform measures on convex bodies are included, and no smoothness or positive curvature is assumed. Through a theorem of Otto and Villani, the same constant also gives a quadratic transport inequality. This resolves Bizeul’s conjecture for centered laws with a density. The constant is not computed: the proof works by contradiction, so it gives no value. The hypothesis controls only linear functions; the conclusion then controls every smooth function. If the log-Sobolev and Poincaré constants coincide, the gradient of a spectral-gap eigenfunction plays this role instead. The key extra information is about prediction. Observe a point through Gaussian noise of variance r, and predict w from the noisy observation. The paper proves that the prediction keeps, in the limit, a fraction e^(−r) of the mean square of w, on a fixed interval of noise levels. The tools are a formula of Guo, Shamai and Verdú for Gaussian channels, and an entropy-loss comparison built from repeated Gaussian observations of independent copies. Symmetry in the many tensor indices forces its pieces to nearly commute, and a Hermite expansion along the diffusion, run for a time t, makes covariance fluctuations follow a lognormal law, e^(√(8t) Z − 4t). Finally, observe a Gaussian vector whose covariance depends on this matrix field. Taking t large gives the contradiction.

5:42Where it stands

The preprint is not peer reviewed and has not been revised. There is no Lean formalization, so the argument rests on the paper alone, and its constant is not explicit. It is not among the papers OSVerify reviewed, and we have seen no expert review. If it holds, uniform Gaussian tails of linear functions suffice for a centered log-concave density to satisfy a log-Sobolev inequality, with a constant independent of dimension, and with it Gaussian concentration for every Lipschitz function. The size of that constant is unknown.
