No hidden rhythm?

A claim that billiards in every triangle with an irrational angle are ergodic, and even weakly mixing

The claim, in plain words

Shoot a frictionless ball inside a triangular table: it travels in straight lines and bounces with the angle in equal to the angle out. If every angle is a rational multiple of π, the walls can only send the ball in finitely many directions (in our runs, 8 for the 45-45-90 table and 12 for 30-60-90), so the ball never forgets which family of directions it started in and the motion is not ergodic. If an angle is an irrational multiple of π, the directions can spread: in a triangle with angles of 1 and √2 radians, each of our trajectories used over 200,000 distinct directions in a million bounces. Ergodic means time averages equal space averages for a typical start. Weak mixing asks for more: no hidden rhythm, no quantity that turns steadily like a clock hand; equivalently, two independent balls watched together are ergodic. A standard example that is ergodic but not weakly mixing is the 45-45-90 table restricted to one typical family of eight directions; in our run the average squared correlation for a corner region stays near 0.09 there, while in the irrational triangle it falls to about 0.005.

As the papers recount, Zemlyakov and Katok unfolded billiards into straight lines on a surface, which for rational polygons is a translation surface. Kerckhoff, Masur and Smillie proved unique ergodicity in almost every direction there, and ergodicity of the full flow for a dense Gδ family of polygons. Vorobets gave explicit ergodic examples, including some irrational right triangles, whose angles have exceptionally fast simultaneous rational approximations. Chaika and Forni proved weak mixing for a dense Gδ set of polygons with any fixed number of sides, which, the papers note, is not the same as almost every triangle. Numerical studies disagreed: some suggested weak mixing or even mixing, others non-ergodicity in some irrational right or symmetric triangles.

Result 150 is two OpenAI preprints. The earlier one (12 pages, September 25, 2026) claims, in its Theorem 1, that in every nondegenerate triangle with at least one angle irrational relative to π the billiard flow is ergodic for area times uniform direction, trajectories hitting a corner being discarded (a null set), with “no genericity or Diophantine condition”. Its Corollary 7 derives, from outside theorems, quantum ergodicity and nodal-domain counts that grow without bound along density-one subsequences of the triangle’s Dirichlet or Neumann eigenfunctions. The later one (13 pages, October 5, 2026) is the headline: in its Theorem 1.1 the same flow is weakly mixing. It holds for all but countably many triangle shapes, including irrational right triangles, and “the irrationality assumption is sharp for the full phase space.”

What it doesn’t claim

Neither paper claims mixing, rates, unique ergodicity, or anything about every individual orbit, and nothing is claimed beyond triangles. Neither calls the problem famous or open, or says who first asked it.

How the proof goes

  1. Double the table. Gluing two copies of the triangle along their sides gives a flat pillow with three cone points, cone angles twice the triangle’s angles, on which bounces become straight motion. Expanding a function of position and direction in Fourier modes of the direction turns motion along and across the trajectory into Cauchy–Riemann derivatives that shift the mode by one. As the papers explain, Forni and Moll had shown that, when a corner angle is irrational, invariant functions with square-integrable derivatives along and across the motion are constant; the difficulty is that the indicator of an invariant set is merely measurable.
  2. Ergodicity. The earlier paper smooths in space away from the corners, shows each Fourier coefficient has a square-integrable gradient, sums every second mode from a fixed index upward so that the flow derivative telescopes to one endpoint term, and uses long averages clear of the corners with two limits in a careful order. Every coefficient is then parallel, without using the irrational angle. Going once around the irrational corner turns directions by twice its angle, which no parallel coefficient of nonzero mode survives; only the average over directions remains, and it is constant.
  3. Weak mixing. The later paper runs the same machinery on an eigenfunction, which the flow multiplies by a phase turning at rate λ, and gets a sharp bound: the total gradient squared is at most λ² times the function squared. The forward derivative uses all of it, so the sideways derivative vanishes and the eigenfunction is a plane wave c(v)eiλx·v. Reflection in either wall through the irrational corner leaves the amplitude unchanged, and together they rotate directions by twice that angle, forcing a constant amplitude; the third wall misses that corner, so reflecting in it shifts the phase by an amount that changes with the direction, impossible unless λ = 0. With only constant eigenfunctions, a standard Hilbert–Schmidt argument makes the product flow ergodic.

The numbers in the video

  • Distinct directions along single trajectories: 8 for the 45-45-90 table and 12 for 30-60-90 (four starts each); for angles of 1 and √2 radians, medians of 394, 3,206, 27,337 and 213,652 after 103, 104, 105 and 106 bounces (eight starts, each over 206,000 at 106).
  • The average over [0, T] of the squared correlation of a corner region (a quarter of the area), 20,000 starting states: 0.095, 0.088 and 0.087 at T = 10, 50 and 200 for the 45-45-90 table restricted to one family of eight directions, against 0.059, 0.018 and 0.0046 for the irrational triangle.
  • In the irrational triangle, the third wall is 0.988 from the corner of angle 1 radian, so the offset in the reflection is 1.976.
  • Numerics cannot prove weak mixing, and the papers themselves contain no numerics; these are our illustrations.

Bounces become straight motion on this surface. 6:08Where it stands

Neither preprint is peer reviewed or revised. The ergodicity theorem is formalized in Lean, in 39 files and about 12,400 lines that also construct the flow itself, with no unproved steps that we found; we have not run the check. The weak mixing theorem has no Lean, although the catalog lists the Lean link under the weak mixing headline. Neither paper is among those OSVerify reviewed, and we have seen no expert review. If it holds, billiards in every triangle with an irrational angle have no hidden rhythm: correlations fade on average over time, though not necessarily at every moment.
