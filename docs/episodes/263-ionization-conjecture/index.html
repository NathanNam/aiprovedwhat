How many electrons can an atom hold?

Three AI preprints claim the ionization conjecture for the full Schrödinger model, and Solovej’s generalized version

The claim, in plain words

A nucleus of charge Z attracts Z electrons and becomes neutral; in nature, at most one more ever sticks. Hydrogen can hold a second electron, but only because of correlation. Real atoms are remarkably uniform: the tabulated first ionization energies of the first 108 elements stay between about 4 electron volts, for caesium, and about 25, for helium, with no growth as atoms get heavier (our reading of the NIST database).

Mathematically, this was a famous open problem. Barry Simon’s 2000 list asked to prove that the number of electrons an atom can bind, minus Z, stays bounded. Earlier proofs allowed far more: Lieb in 1984, fewer than 2Z plus the number of nuclei; Nam in 2012, about 1.22 Z. In 2025 Lewin wrote that no proof existed even with a constant like 10¹⁰⁰. Solovej proved the bounded version in 2003 for Hartree–Fock theory, an approximation.

Result 263 is three OpenAI preprints, 134 pages, dated September 24, 2026. The main paper, 84 pages, claims that in the full nonrelativistic Schrödinger model, with fermions of two spin states and fixed nuclei of charge at least one, a molecule of total nuclear charge Z strictly binds at most Z + C × (number of nuclei) electrons, with C universal. It also claims the first ionization energies of neutral atoms are bounded above and below by universal positive constants. Two companions claim Solovej’s generalized ionization conjecture: the energy to remove m electrons follows the Thomas–Fermi law, a constant times m^(7/3), as m and Z/m grow, and the outer radii follow the matching Thomas–Fermi law.

What it doesn’t claim

The sharp bound of one or two extra electrons; any explicit value of C, which comes from an argument by contradiction; bosons, where the statement is false; relativity; or the limit of the first ionization energy.

Last checked October 30, 2026. The preprints have not been revised. Lean formalizes the two asymptotic theorems, about 95,000 and 77,000 lines with no unproved steps, which we did not build. The headline theorems of the main paper, bounded excess and the ionization-energy bounds, are not formalized. OSVerify has not reviewed them, and we found no specialist comment.

How the proofs go

  1. Solovej’s architecture. Compare the true atom with a screened Thomas–Fermi model at every scale, from the nucleus out, and show that the outermost electrons see an almost neutral core.
  2. The full problem. The new work makes each comparison hold for the full many-body Schrödinger problem, where electrons are correlated, and runs the whole argument by contradiction. That is why C is never computed.
