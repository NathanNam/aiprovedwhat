Both finite, still unequal?

The claim, in plain words

Free probability has its own entropy, and Voiculescu defined it twice. The microstates entropy χ counts matrix approximations: how much volume of large matrix tuples reproduces the joint moments. The nonmicrostates entropy χ* uses free Fisher information, measuring how the tuple spreads under free semicircular noise. For one variable they agree. Biane, Capitaine and Guionnet proved in 2003 that χ never exceeds χ*. Voiculescu’s unification problem asked whether the two always agree, and in 2004 Guionnet conjectured equality whenever χ is finite. The 2020 refutation of Connes’ embedding conjecture already gives tuples, once blurred by a little semicircular noise, with no matrix approximations, where χ is minus infinity while χ* is finite; so a gap was known, but only with χ infinite.

Result 298 is one OpenAI paper, 15 pages, dated September 25, 2026, and published October 6. It claims a bounded self-adjoint tuple in a tracial von Neumann algebra with both entropies finite and χ at least one half below χ*: a negative answer to the finite-entropy form. The paper’s explicit choice uses 264 + 1 variables, and it does not address smaller numbers.

What it doesn’t claim

Anything about free entropy dimension, about Connes embedding, or about whether the limsup and liminf versions of χ agree. Because χ is finite, this example does have matrix approximations, unlike the Connes embedding examples.

How the proof goes

  1. Atoms that force blocks. Take p free semicircular variables and one more variable with p equally spaced atoms, in a separate tensor factor so that it commutes with all of them, and blur everything with a little free semicircular noise. Matrix approximations must be nearly block-diagonal, and a detectable trace of that structure survives added noise.
  2. One unit lost, half a unit kept. A random matrix shows that structure with probability at most e−4d², which costs at least one unit of entropy, while on the free side χ* stays close to the semicircle benchmark; a heat-flow comparison links the two sides and leaves a gap of at least one half.

The numbers in the video

  • Ours: for the variance-one uniform law, χ* = 1.41138 and χ = 1.41139, just below the semicircle’s maximum of 1.41894; Weyl’s volume formula for the semicircle gives 1.4188 at matrix size 3,000.
  • Ours: with twelve atoms, matrix models keep about 11 units of extra block energy over a random matrix with the same variances, up to noise time 100.
  • Ours: the paper’s inequalities hold at its parameters; its method’s constants need p of at least about 1.7 × 1017, a limit of the method, not of the problem.

1:25The claim

Result 298 is one OpenAI paper, 15 pages, dated September 25 and published October 6, 2026. It claims a bounded self-adjoint tuple in a tracial von Neumann algebra with both entropies finite and χ at least 1/2 below χ*: a negative answer to the finite-entropy form. The paper’s explicit choice uses 2⁶⁴ + 1 variables, and it does not address smaller numbers. Not claimed: anything about free entropy dimension, about Connes embedding, or about whether the limsup and liminf versions of χ agree. 2:10How the proof goes

The construction takes p free semicircular variables and one more variable with p equally spaced atoms, placed in a separate tensor factor so that it commutes with all of them, then blurs everything with a little free semicircular noise. On the matrix side, approximations must be nearly block-diagonal, and a detectable trace of that structure survives added noise. Our computer sees it with 12 atoms: the evolved matrices keep block energy near 12 + t, while a random matrix with the same variances shows only 1 + t, up to t = 100. A random matrix shows that structure with probability at most e^(−4d²), which costs at least 1 unit of entropy, while on the free side, after the extra noise, sparse cumulants keep χ* within 0.0052 of the semicircle benchmark; a heat-flow comparison links the two sides and leaves a gap of at least 1/2. Our computer checks the paper’s inequalities at its parameters; the method’s constants need p of at least about 2 × 10¹⁷, a limit of the method, not of the problem.

3:22Where it stands

The paper is unrefereed and unchanged since release. In Lean, the main theorem is formalized with Voiculescu’s definitions written out and matches the paper, in about 15,600 lines; our scan finds no unproved steps, but we did not build it. The only expert reaction we found is from David Jekel on Bluesky, who called it novel and relatively short but badly written in many ways, with citations missing, and said this negative answer was what Jekel expected. If it holds, Voiculescu’s two free entropies can differ even when both are finite, for at least one tuple of 2⁶⁴ + 1 variables.
