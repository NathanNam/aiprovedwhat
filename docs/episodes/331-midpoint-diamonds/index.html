Midpoints aren’t enough?

Seven AI preprints claim a reflexive space with midpoint but no uniform asymptotic convexity

The claim, in plain words

Stand at a unit vector and step a distance t in some direction far out in the space. Does the length grow? A norm is asymptotically uniformly convex if every far-out step gains a definite amount. A weaker property, asymptotic midpoint uniform convexity, asks only that a step and its opposite gain on average. On a tiny tree from the release’s space, our computer shows the gap: one push gains nothing until t reaches 1, while the opposite push grows like the √(1 + t²).

Diamond graphs ask a related question: replace each edge by many two-edge paths, again and again, and see how badly the result must be distorted to fit in a space. In 2017, seven authors showed that midpoint convexity forces the distortion of countably branching diamonds to grow without bound, and noted that a 2016 question was still open: can midpoint convexity always be upgraded to the stronger property by changing the norm? In 2025 a book asked, as Problem 39, whether a reflexive space with no such upgrade must always fit the diamonds with bounded distortion, and its authors suspected yes. On September 18, 2026, Baudier answered the upgrade question no, in a non-reflexive space, with a proof that the note says was generated by an OpenAI model.

Result 331 is seven OpenAI papers, 225 pages in all, dated September 27 and made public October 6, 2026. The headline paper claims a separable reflexive space, built from James’s segment norm on a forest of countably branching trees, that is asymptotically midpoint uniformly convex with an explicit modulus, but has no equivalent asymptotically uniformly convex norm. In the same space, depth-k diamonds need distortion at least √(1 + k/12); a companion paper improves this to √(1 + k/4).

What it doesn’t claim

Not claimed: the first separation of the two properties, which Baudier had already done; optimal constants; or any upper bound on diamond distortion. Some companion spaces are not reflexive.

How the proof goes

  1. Tall trees defeat every AUC norm. Inside each tree, root paths all have length 1, and stepping to a child is a push in a far-out direction. If some equivalent norm were asymptotically uniformly convex, each step to a far-out child could gain a fixed factor, so a path down a tall enough tree would grow without bound, yet root paths stay bounded. Since the forest has trees of every height, no equivalent norm can be asymptotically uniformly convex.
  2. Midpoints force the squared stretch to grow. The midpoint estimate tests the two pushes together, with a correction placed at the first vertices outside a finite head; the correction cancels when the two sides are averaged. Then, for each diamond edge, its many midpoints force the squared stretch to grow by a fixed amount, 1/12, per level, and summing over k levels gives the bound. Our computer tabulates it: √(1 + k/12) reaches 2 at depth 36, while the companion bound reaches 2 at depth 12.

The numbers in the video

  • Ours, exact on a tiny tree: one push gives length max(1, t), the opposite push √(1 + t²).
  • The bounds: √(1 + k/12) reaches 2 at depth 36; the companion’s √(1 + k/4) reaches 2 at depth 12 (our arithmetic).
  • Ours: the diamond distance facts the proof uses hold for branching 2, 3 and 4 up to depth 5.
  • Lean: 7 checks; 171 files and 49,376 lines in the import closures (counted by us; not run by us).
