Stem 322 survives?

An AI preprint claims to settle the Kervaire invariant problem at the prime three

The claim, in plain words

At the prime two, the Kervaire invariant problem asks in which dimensions a framed manifold can have Kervaire invariant one. Browder turned it into whether certain classes, h_j², survive the Adams spectral sequence, and the answer is dimensions 2, 6, 14, 30, 62 and 126 only: Mahowald and Tangora settled 30, Barratt, Jones and Mahowald 62, Hill, Hopkins and Ravenel ruled out everything beyond 126 in 2016, and a 2024 computer-assisted preprint by Lin, Wang and Xu settled 126. At odd primes there is an analogous family, the classes b_j on the Adams two-line, a homotopy question rather than one about manifolds; at the prime three their stems are 10, 34, 106, 322, 970 and on. The first survives; Toda showed the second dies at every odd prime; Ravenel showed in 1978 that at primes 5 and up all but the first die, and noted that at three the class in stem 106 survives. Ravenel’s book still says the fate of the higher ones at three is unknown.

Result 309 is one OpenAI paper, 90 pages, dated September 24, 2026, and made public October 6. It claims that at the prime three, exactly the standard classes with indices 0, 2 and 3 survive, in stems 10, 106 and 322, and that each surviving coset contains an element of order exactly 3. The new parts are that stem 322 survives and that every class from stem 970 on dies. Along the way it proves a conjecture of Belmont and Ray about a symmetry of order 9, refutes one clause of another conjecture as they stated it, and draws unstable consequences.

What it doesn’t claim

Other primes, anything about manifolds, the whole homotopy group in stem 322, or that every element of the coset has order 3.

The topologist Kyle Ormsby noted on Bluesky that the 90-page computation contains no charts; that is a remark on presentation, not a verdict on correctness.

How the proof goes

  1. Killing the higher classes. The paper detects them in a homotopy fixed point spectrum of Morava E-theory with a cyclic group of order 9. Euler class bounds make the localized spectral sequences die out by differentials of length at most 157 or 469, and the known survivor in stem 106 forces a differential of length 93, which forces every higher detector to support a differential too. The stem-322 detector has a different key, so this argument does not touch it.
  2. Building the stem-322 class. A Toda-style construction built from the stem-106 class lands in stem 3 × 108 − 2 = 322, and is then corrected, using a large finite linear-algebra calculation; the paper reports eliminations such as about 137,000 equations of rank about 23,000, but supplies no code.

The numbers in the video

  • Ours: the stems from 970 on are all two less than a multiple of 972, and their detectors have key 0 mod 9, while the stem-322 detector has key 6.
  • Ours: we rebuilt the mod-3 cobar complex from scratch and reproduced the paper’s small checkpoints exactly: dimensions 12 and 470, ranks 11 and 456, leaving a three-dimensional group. That checks only the small numbers, not the large calculation or the topology.

For the higher classes, the paper detects them in a homotopy fixed point spectrum of Morava E-theory with a cyclic group of order 9. Euler class bounds make the localized spectral sequences die out by differentials of length at most 157 or 469, and the known survivor in stem 106 forces a differential of length 93, which forces every higher detector to support a differential too. Our computer checks the bookkeeping: the stems from 970 on are all two less than a multiple of 972, and their detectors have key 0 mod 9, while the stem 322 detector has key 6, so this argument does not touch it. For stem 322, a Toda-style construction built from the stem 106 class lands in stem 3 × 108 − 2, and is then corrected, using a large finite linear-algebra calculation. The paper reports eliminations such as about 137,000 equations of rank about 23,000, but supplies no code. Our computer rebuilt the mod-3 cobar complex from scratch and reproduced the paper’s small checkpoints exactly: dimensions 12 and 470, ranks 11 and 456, leaving a three-dimensional group. That checks only the small numbers, not the large calculation or the topology.
