Spaces as algebra?

An AI preprint claims Grothendieck’s homotopy hypothesis for every coherator in the Ara–Henry convention

The claim, in plain words

In 1983, in Pursuing Stacks, Grothendieck proposed that spaces, up to homotopy, are the same thing as purely algebraic ∞-groupoids: points, paths between points, homotopies between paths, and so on forever, with composition, inverses and every coherence law supplied by operations rather than by geometry. Maltsiniotis later made this precise with coherators, theories built stage by stage by freely adding fillers for parallel pairs of operations. Strict versions are too rigid: by the Eckmann–Hilton argument two compatible operations collapse into one commutative operation, which is why strict groupoids cannot model even the 2-sphere. In 2016 Henry reduced the hypothesis to one gluing step: attach a new cell parallel to an existing one, plus a cell joining them, and show that nothing changes up to homotopy. In 2022 Henry wrote that this sounds like it should be very easy, but is still open at this point. Only dimension 3 was known, for groupoids of one particular kind, through work of Lanari and of Henry and Lanari.

Result 312 is one OpenAI paper, 26 pages, dated September 24, 2026, and made public October 6. It claims that Henry’s gluing step is a weak equivalence for every coherator in the Ara–Henry convention, where each generating filler’s input has dimension at most its output. Through Henry’s 2016 theorem, this gives a left semi-model structure and a Quillen equivalence with spaces: the homotopy hypothesis for these groupoids.

What it doesn’t claim

Coherators without that dimension bound, strict groupoids, higher categories that are not groupoids, other models such as Batanin’s, or a full model structure. The step to spaces depends on Henry’s 98-page 2016 preprint, which, as far as we found, has not appeared in a journal.

The semi-model structure and the comparison with spaces are not formalized; a third party’s claim to have formalized the rest has not been checked. OSVerify has not reviewed it, and we found no specialist assessment. One public doubt was raised: on the algebraic topology mailing list on October 9, Johnathon Taylor, whose own paper the release cites for context, wrote that “there is definitely not elementary expansions in the sense the paper lays out”. They gave no argument, and no one followed up on the mathematics.

How the proof goes

  1. From the top down. The proof tests weak equivalence by lifting problems with exact boundaries. It first cuts the models off above some dimension q, forcing unique fillers there, then runs a descending induction from q down to 0. At each step, the gluing results already proved in higher dimensions make certain tree and cylinder models contractible, and this lets the paper build a path-object-like construction that interprets every operation of the coherator. A retract argument finishes the step, and the cutoff is removed at the end, because each test only involves finitely many dimensions.
  2. What Lean checks. The core gluing theorem, not the semi-model structure or the comparison with spaces. One footnote misquotes Henry’s definition, without affecting the argument.
