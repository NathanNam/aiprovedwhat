Dead over F, alive on A?

Two preprints claim the first integral counterexamples to Gersten’s conjecture: nonzero K-theory classes on ramified regular local rings that vanish over the fraction field

The claim, in plain words

Algebraic K-theory attaches a sequence of groups, K0, K1, K2 and so on, to a ring. Take a regular local ring A, the algebraic version of a smooth point, and its fraction field F. Gersten’s conjecture, from 1973, says that the K-groups of A sit inside those of F, and more: a whole sequence of maps out of them is exact. The first part says a class that vanishes over F was already zero on A.

As the papers tell it, the conjecture was proved for rings containing a field, by Quillen for geometric rings and by Panin in general; and with finite coefficients for rings smooth over a discrete valuation ring, and for unramified rings. In mixed characteristic, where the ring has characteristic zero but its residue field has characteristic p, the general integral case stayed open. In August 2026 Feld found a counterexample with coefficients mod three, in degree two; he noted it does not contradict the integral conjecture.

Result 209 bundles two OpenAI preprints. Each gives a two-dimensional regular local ring of mixed characteristic (0, 5) and an integral K-theory class on it that is not zero, yet vanishes over the fraction field. An integral counterexample to Gersten’s conjecture (25 pages, September 25, 2026) works in degree five, on the surface 5 + x4 + y4 = 0 over a ring of 5-adic integers. An integral degree-three Gersten counterexample (10 pages, September 26, 2026) works in degree three, on a quintic surface over the 5-adic integers with fifth roots of unity. So, if they are right, the unrestricted integral conjecture fails.

What it doesn’t claim

Anything about rings that contain a field, rings smooth over a discrete valuation ring, or unramified rings; the version with rational coefficients; the order of these classes; or the smallest degree that fails. Both rings are ramified: 5 lies deep inside the maximal ideal.

A 2015–2020 preprint by Satoshi Mochizuki, one version of which claimed Gersten’s conjecture for all regular local rings, has a general vanishing theorem that would rule out such classes; the degree-five paper points to a gap in one of its lemmas, and if the new papers are right, that theorem cannot hold as stated.

How the proof goes

  1. Plant a class on a curve. A class pushed forward from a curve through the point dies automatically over the fraction field. The hard part is showing it is not zero on A.
  2. Degree five. The class starts over the cyclotomic integers with five inverted, then moves to the ring. After an extension, the curve splits into four points on the Fermat quartic. If the class vanished, a relation among these points would become a principal divisor on the special fiber. Mapped to the elliptic curve w2 = s4 + 1, the four points land at +Q, −Q, −Q, +Q, with weights 1, 4, 4, 1; modulo five the sum is 4Q, not zero (we checked this), while the plain sum of weights, 1 + 4 + 4 + 1, would have been zero modulo five.
  3. Degree three. The class is a Bott element times a uniformizer on the curve y = 0; a Steinberg relation makes it integral. To show it survives, the paper compares norms modulo five: the planted curve has residue degree one and contributes one, while every other point that could cancel it has residue degree divisible by five and contributes zero.

The numbers in the video

  • Point counts on w2 = s4 + 1 (ours, brute force): 4, 32, 640 and 391,680 over the fields with 5, 25, 625 and 58 elements, matching the paper’s recursion; the four points land at +Q, −Q, −Q, +Q, and the weighted sum is 4Q modulo 5.
  • The degree-three model (ours): its special fibre is singular at the point, and the other special-fibre points appear only over the field with 55 elements.
