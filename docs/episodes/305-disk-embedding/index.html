Free groups not good?

The claims, in plain words

In dimension five and up, the Whitney trick says that if the intersection points of two submanifolds of complementary dimension cancel in pairs algebraically, a disc can push each pair apart. That trick drives surgery and the h-cobordism theorem. In dimension four the Whitney disc can itself hit the surfaces. Freedman rescued the trick in 1982, for topological discs, when the fundamental group is trivial, and work of Freedman and Quinn, Freedman and Teichner, and Krushkal and Quinn extended it to so-called good groups, including finite, solvable and subexponential-growth groups. The central question became whether free groups are good. In 1983 Freedman proposed, with cautious neutrality, that the Whitehead double of the Borromean rings is not slice, which would make the disc theorem fail for free groups; later experts called the question open. Separately, in 1979, Wall asked whether every Poincaré duality group is the fundamental group of a closed aspherical manifold; Davis answered no in 1998, with groups that are not finitely presented.

Result 305 is three OpenAI papers, 133 pages, dated September 24, 2026, and made public October 6. Two of them independently claim a counterexample to the unrestricted disc embedding conjecture: a compact smooth four-manifold with discs and framed dual spheres meeting every algebraic condition, whose boundary circles still bound no disjoint locally flat discs (in the second paper, none respecting the boundary framings). One of them deduces, through a theorem of Powell, Ray and Teichner, that the free group on two letters is not good, which, if correct, goes the way Freedman guessed; so unrestricted topological surgery and the five-dimensional s-cobordism theorem cannot both hold. The third paper, which builds on one of the first two, claims a finitely presented Poincaré duality group of dimension four, with a finite classifying space, that is not the fundamental group of any closed aspherical topological four-manifold.

What they don’t claim

Which of surgery or s-cobordism fails; anything that settles Freedman’s named test cases, such as the Whitehead double of the Borromean rings; anything about homology manifolds; or anything in higher dimensions.

How the proof goes

  1. Borromean algebra. The construction installs Brunnian triple commutators along grope boundaries, the algebra of the Borromean rings, three circles linked together though no two are linked. Disjoint discs would let you cut the manifold along three-dimensional walls that miss them, where these commutators become relations and force the flat SL₂(ℂ) deformations to obey a cube-zero law.
  2. Algebra in characteristic ℓ. Chern–Simons potentials turn this into exact algebraic identities, reduced modulo a large prime, and a finite algebraic theorem rules them out once there are at least five walls. The third paper uses Davis’s reflection trick on a chamber whose marked boundary has no filling shaped like a graph.

3:03How the proof goes

The construction installs Brunnian triple commutators along grope boundaries, the algebra of the Borromean rings, three circles linked together though no two are linked. Our computer checks the model: all three pairwise linking numbers are zero, and the commutator of length 10 dies as soon as any one of its three letters is erased everywhere. Disjoint discs would let you cut the manifold along three-dimensional walls that miss them, where these commutators become relations and force the flat SL₂(ℂ) deformations to obey a cube-zero law. Chern–Simons potentials turn this into exact algebraic identities, reduced modulo a large prime, and a finite algebraic theorem rules them out once there are at least 5 walls. The construction is huge: by our count from the first paper’s definitions, about 10¹⁴ discs. The third paper uses Davis’s reflection trick on a chamber whose marked boundary has no filling shaped like a graph; our computer shows the trick on a right-angled pentagon, where 32 copies glue into a surface of genus 5.

4:12Where it stands

All three papers are unrefereed and unchanged since release, and there is no Lean formalization. In the first 12 days we found no public specialist comment and no reported error. The papers also do not settle the specific test cases Freedman named, such as the Whitehead double of the Borromean rings. If they hold, the four-dimensional disc embedding theorem genuinely needs a restriction on the fundamental group: nonabelian free groups are not good, and in dimension 4 a finitely presented Poincaré duality group need not be the fundamental group of a closed aspherical manifold.
