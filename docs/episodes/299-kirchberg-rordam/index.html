No characters, then Z-stable?

An AI preprint claims the Kirchberg–Rørdam character criterion for Jiang–Su stability

The claim, in plain words

A character of a C*-algebra is a one-dimensional representation, a homomorphism onto the complex numbers; matrix algebras of size two or more have none. Kirchberg’s central sequence algebra collects the sequences that asymptotically commute with the whole algebra, and 𝒵-stability, absorbing the Jiang–Su algebra, is one of the regularity properties from episode 291. In 2014 Kirchberg and Rørdam asked whether a unital separable algebra is 𝒵-stable exactly when its central sequence algebra has no characters; they called one direction, 𝒵-stable implies no characters, trivially true. They showed the question equivalent to one about infinite tensor powers, implicit in work of Dadarlat and Toms, and a problem list still carried it, unsolved, in May 2026.

Result 299 is one OpenAI paper, 21 pages, dated September 25, 2026, and published October 6. It claims that a nonzero unital separable C*-algebra is 𝒵-stable exactly when its central sequence algebra has no characters, for every free ultrafilter; the new part is the hard direction. Its key step: every nonzero unital algebra without characters receives a unital image of the dimension-drop algebra I(2,3) in some finite maximal tensor power of itself. So the infinite tensor power of such a separable algebra is 𝒵-stable, answering the Dadarlat–Toms form of the question.

What it doesn’t claim

That any finite tensor power is 𝒵-stable, a bound on the power, or anything for non-unital algebras.

How the proof goes

  1. The target. The dimension-drop algebra I(2,3) has irreducible representations of sizes 2, 6 and 3, and no characters; Kirchberg and Rørdam had shown that a unital map of it into the central sequence algebra is all that is needed.
  2. A cube. The paper builds the map on a cube of coordinates, sliding a copy of the 2×2 matrices from one tensor leg to the next. The cost is fast-growing exponents: for a comparison constant of 3 the bookkeeping uses 11 copies of an earlier tensor power, and for 10, 10,004.

0:00The question

A character of a C*-algebra is a one-dimensional representation, a homomorphism onto the complex numbers. Matrix algebras of size two or more have none: our computer checks that −i/2, times the commutator of two Pauli matrices, times the third, is the identity, so a character would have to send 1 to 0. A direct sum of matrix blocks has exactly one character for each 1×1 block. Kirchberg’s central sequence algebra collects the sequences that asymptotically commute with the whole algebra. Our computer shows them in ten tensored copies of 2×2 matrices: a copy of σ_z placed further along commutes better and better with a fixed element whose weights halve along the legs, while two such sequences need not commute with each other. The dimension-drop algebra I(2,3) has irreducible representations of sizes 2, 6 and 3, and no characters; Kirchberg and Rørdam had shown that a unital map of it into the central sequence algebra is all that is needed.
