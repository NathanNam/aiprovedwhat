Shallow circuits, no parity, proved at last?

Two AI preprints claim that parity is not in QAC⁰, Moore’s 1999 conjecture

The claim, in plain words

Parity asks whether a string of bits has an odd number of ones, so every bit matters. In the 1980s, Ajtai, Furst, Saxe and Sipser, and then Håstad, proved that classical circuits of constant depth and polynomial size, with NOT gates and unlimited AND and OR gates, cannot compute it.

Quantum circuits of constant depth and polynomial size, with one-qubit gates and giant Toffoli gates, form the class called QAC⁰. In 1999 Cristopher Moore conjectured that they cannot copy a bit onto many wires, which is equivalent to computing parity: Hadamard gates turn one into the other. With a fan-out gate, parity takes depth 3 for any number of bits; without it, a tree of CNOT gates needs a new layer every time the number of bits doubles. Partial results piled up, with lower bounds for few extra qubits and for depths 2, 3 and 4. In 2024 a claimed proof was withdrawn within a week, and four days after the new papers’ date a human preprint still called the general case open.

Result 274 is two OpenAI preprints, 48 pages, both dated September 24, 2026, with independent proofs. They claim that for any fixed depth and polynomially many qubits, with extra qubits starting at zero, one qubit measured and everything else discarded, no circuit gets parity right on every input with any fixed advantage over guessing, once the number of bits is large enough. Using reductions by Xu and Li, majority is ruled out too.

What it doesn’t claim

Circuits with fan-out gates or intermediate measurements, or with more than polynomially many qubits, where depth 7 circuits are known to approximate parity. The threshold on the number of bits is not explicit.

Last checked October 17, 2026. The preprints have not been revised. The first paper’s main theorem is formalized in Lean at full strength, about 3,600 lines with no unproved steps, which we did not build; the majority result is not. OSVerify has not reviewed them. Scott Aaronson called the problem “one of the great questions of quantum complexity theory since 1999”, but we found no technical comment from specialists.

How the proofs go

  1. Mirrors and mismatches. The first proof treats every Toffoli as a reflection about a product state. Its key structural theorem bounds how far a shallow stack of such reflections can push a product pattern: many mismatches are astronomically unlikely. Parity needs exactly the opposite, a transition from every input qubit agreeing to every input qubit disagreeing. Where polynomial approximations fail, the proof runs the circuit backwards at a larger scale, through a finite chain of scales.
  2. A second, independent proof. The second paper prunes gates that almost never fire and follows regular trajectories.

The numbers in the video

  • Ours: with a fan-out gate, parity in depth 3 for any number of bits; a CNOT tree needs depth ⌈log₂ n⌉.
  • Ours: one Toffoli touching every input has correlation with parity of only 21−n, like a classical AND gate.
  • Ours: the identity behind the first proof (parity as an average transition between opposite product states), checked for 3 to 5 bits.
