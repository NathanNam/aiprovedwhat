One bound state, always, proved at last?

Two AI preprints on the bound states of the BFSS matrix model

The claim, in plain words

In 1996, Banks, Fischler, Shenker and Susskind proposed that M-theory, a candidate theory of quantum gravity, could be described by quantum mechanics of very large matrices: nine N×N matrices for N particles called D0-branes, with sixteen supersymmetries. For the idea to work, the N particles must form exactly one bound state with zero energy, standing for a graviton carrying N units of momentum.

That is delicate. The potential has flat valleys where the matrices commute, so particles can drift apart for free, and the energies form a continuum starting at zero. The bound state has to sit exactly at the edge of that continuum. The original paper said a direct proof had not yet been given. Index calculations in the late 1990s made it convincing, 5/4 − 1/4 = 1 state for two particles in ten dimensions and a net count of zero in four and six, but a full proof stayed open; a 2021 paper still called it an open problem.

Result 270 is two OpenAI preprints, 57 pages, dated September 24 and October 5, 2026. The first claims that for every N of two or more, the model with the centre of mass removed has exactly one normalizable zero-energy state, and that it is rotation invariant. For two particles, existence and uniqueness had already been argued; the new content is three or more. The second claims that for two particles there are also infinitely many normalizable states with positive energy, sitting inside the continuum. That contradicts a sentence of the original paper: “No other normalizable bound states can occur.” The preprint notes that the large-N conjecture is a separate question.

What it doesn’t claim

Anything about large N, gravity or M-theory itself, other numbers of dimensions, or the values of the positive energies.

How the proofs go

  1. Counting with partitions. Switching on a mass deformation gives exactly one ground state per partition of N, a way of writing N as a sum of whole numbers: 5 for N = 4, 42 for N = 10. As the deformation is removed, every partition with two or more parts drifts apart into separate clusters, each a smaller bound state known by induction. That leaves room for exactly one state at the centre, and forces it to exist.
  2. Trapped by spinning. To escape, the matrices must line up along one direction, which cannot carry rotation in two planes at once, so states spinning fast enough in two planes are trapped. By our estimate of the paper’s constants, the argument covers only very fast spinning sectors, with second spin label above about 37. That says nothing either way about lower sectors.

The numbers in the video

  • Ours: a two-variable toy without fermions has a discrete spectrum (lowest energy 1.108); with fermions added, the lowest energy falls toward zero as the box grows, from 0.40 to 0.025.
  • Ours: the index arithmetic 5/4 − 1/4 = 1 and 1/4 − 1/4 = 0, and the partition counts 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, …, 42.
  • Ours: the second paper’s constants made explicit: C ≈ 13.06 and M = 6, so its argument applies above a second spin label of about 37.
