The claim, in plain words

Take a free group and impose one equation. Some of these one-relator groups are tame, like a surface group of genus two, or a free group in which one generator is forced to have finite order. Others are wild. In the Baumslag–Solitar group BS(1,2), where t a t⁻¹ = a², powers of a get squeezed: a¹⁰²⁴ is a word of length only 20, and the sphere of radius 20 holds 441,070 elements (our computation).

Hyperbolic groups are the negatively curved ones: thin triangles, and loops whose filling area grows only linearly with their length. Baumslag–Solitar subgroups rule that out. Around 1989 Steve Gersten asked whether, for one-relator groups, these subgroups are the only obstruction. The question became known as Gersten’s conjecture. For groups with a finite classifying space the answer is no, by a 2023 example, but for one relation it stayed open, and a 2025 survey listed it as a conjecture. Earlier work settled many cases: Newman and Wise for relators that are proper powers, Louder and Wilton and then Linton for relators far from primitive, and Mutanguha for ascending extensions of free groups. Linton also reduced the whole conjecture to a family of two-generator groups.

Result 258 is two OpenAI preprints, both dated September 25, 2026. The first, 53 pages, claims Gersten’s conjecture: a finitely generated one-relator group with no Baumslag–Solitar subgroup is hyperbolic. The second, 145 pages, claims that every hyperbolic one-relator group is virtually compact special. A finite-index subgroup then sits inside a right-angled Artin group, so these groups are residually finite and linear over the integers, and with Kielak and Linton’s work they are virtually free-by-cyclic.

What it doesn’t claim

An algorithm that decides hyperbolicity; anything for one-relator groups that do contain Baumslag–Solitar subgroups; or anything for groups with more relations, where the statement can fail. In free-by-cyclic, the free normal subgroup may have infinite rank.

How the proofs go

  1. The Magnus hierarchy. Rewrite the group around a letter whose exponent sum is zero, after a trick if there is none, and the relator gets shorter. Baumslag’s group goes from a relator of length 9 to 5 to 3, and at the middle stage a Baumslag–Solitar group appears inside (our computation).
  2. Hyperbolicity. The first paper climbs down Linton’s hierarchy using a new combination theorem for certain extensions. That theorem is proved by contradiction, with measures on infinite words and Brinkmann’s theorem that free-group automorphisms with nothing periodic stretch everything.
  3. Specialness. The second paper builds special quotients of Linton’s two-generator groups and lifts them back with a new filling criterion. It relies on the first paper.

Even our simple tests leave 55 of the 84 kinds of two-generator relator of length 8 undecided.

3:30Where it stands

Both preprints are unrefereed and unchanged since release. Neither is formalized in Lean, and OSVerify has not reviewed them. We found no comment on this result from specialists in one-relator groups. The arguments build heavily on Marco Linton’s earlier reductions. If they hold, a single relation can stop negative curvature in only one way, a Baumslag–Solitar subgroup; and once a one-relator group is negatively curved, it is among the best behaved groups known.
