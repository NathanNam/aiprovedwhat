Exactly one half?

Two AI preprints claim the radius of comparison is half the mean dimension, the Phillips–Toms conjecture

The claims, in plain words

Mean dimension, introduced by Gromov and developed by Lindenstrauss and Weiss, measures how many real dimensions a dynamical system gains per time step. For the shift on sequences of points in a d-dimensional cube it is d. It is not entropy: the coin-flip shift has entropy log 2 but mean dimension 0. The radius of comparison, introduced by Toms in 2006, measures how far a C*-algebra is from strict comparison. For a minimal homeomorphism, the crossed product links the two, and Phillips and Toms conjectured that its radius of comparison is half the mean dimension, a factor suggested by vector bundles: a complex bundle of rank r has real dimension 2r. Niu proved the upper bound, and Hirshberg and Phillips had lower bounds that, for certain special subshifts, come within one of the conjectured value.

Result 302 is two OpenAI papers, 49 pages, dated September 25, 2026, and published October 6. The main paper claims that for every minimal homeomorphism of an infinite compact metrizable space, the radius of comparison of the crossed product equals half the mean dimension, including the case where both are infinite; the new part is the lower bound. Its corollary: zero mean dimension, the small boundary property, 𝒵-stability, finite nuclear dimension and nuclear dimension at most one are all equivalent. Most links were known; the new one is that 𝒵-stability, or finite nuclear dimension, forces mean dimension zero, which Elliott and Niu had claimed in 2025 before a gap was found.

What they don’t claim

Anything about actions of groups other than the integers, or about non-minimal systems.

How the proof goes

  1. An engine from algebraic topology. The main paper turns a comparison bound into a bundle embedding of less than twice the rank. The companion paper’s compression theorem in complex cobordism, built on quantitative forms of the Devinatz–Hopkins–Smith nilpotence theorem due to Mathew and Burklund, turns that embedding into boundary slots in a coloured cover of orbit blocks. Each boundary slot deletes a colour, so the cover becomes thinner than its growth rate allows: a contradiction.
  2. The 1/2, and the budget. The 1/2 appears as the limit of p/(2p + 2). With a comparison bound of 0.4 against a cover rate of 1, the smallest packets have 12 colours, and each window must hold over 100,000 orbit blocks (our computation of the paper’s inequalities).
