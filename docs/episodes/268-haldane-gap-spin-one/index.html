A gap for spin one, proved at last?

Two AI preprints claim the Haldane gap for the spin-one Heisenberg chain

The claim, in plain words

Take a chain of quantum spins, each coupled to its neighbours so that they prefer to point opposite ways: the Heisenberg antiferromagnet. Spin one half was known to be gapless: its lowest excitations cost almost nothing on a long chain. In the early 1980s Duncan Haldane predicted that for spin one, and integer spins generally, there should be a gap, a fixed minimum energy however long the chain. Affleck, Kennedy, Lieb and Tasaki proved a gap in 1987 for a modified spin-one chain, but the pure Heisenberg chain resisted. In 2025 Tasaki wrote that a rigorous proof seemed far beyond reach, and called it a challenge to find a strategy for a computer-aided proof.

Result 268 is two OpenAI preprints, 58 pages, dated September 24, 2026. The first claims the conjecture in a finite-volume form. For the pure spin-one Heisenberg chain on even periodic rings of 60 sites or more, the ground state is unique and the gap is larger than (4/105)·log(80/79) ≈ 0.0005; from 2,304 sites on, larger than log(20)/784 ≈ 0.004. The second treats odd open chains with a field of 3/5 applied at both ends. It proves a gap above log(10)/392 ≈ 0.006 for long enough chains, and boundary-selected infinite states with Tasaki’s twist index −1. That gap is exactly the assumption Tasaki made in 2025, with field 3/5 instead of the suggested value 1.

What it doesn’t claim

The actual size of the gap, which these bounds undershoot by a factor of 70 or more; odd rings, free ends or other fields; spin 2 and higher; or uniqueness of the infinite-volume ground state.

How the proofs go

  1. One partition function, two distributions. The proof writes the thermal partition function in two ways: as a sum over energy levels, and as the trace of a spatial transfer operator built from a bond expansion of Aizenman and Nachtergaele. Each gives a probability distribution. Doubling the chain length or the inverse temperature squares one of them and makes it more concentrated, and exact identities link the two. Once both are concentrated, the leftover error shrinks doubly exponentially, and a gap follows.
  2. Seeded by exact computation. The seeds are traces in integer arithmetic with proven error bounds, on twisted rings of up to 12 sites, and a matrix-product trial state at 72 and 120 sites whose energy beats a threshold by about 10−7 per site.

The numbers in the video

  • Ours: exact diagonalization of spin-one rings: gap 1 at 4 sites, 0.432 at 18, extrapolating to about 0.41 (accepted value 0.4105). Spin-one-half rings: the gap keeps falling, 0.183 at 24 sites.
  • Ours: the spin profile of a 13-site open chain with field 3/5 at both ends.
  • Ours: the paper’s bootstrap table reproduces exactly in rational arithmetic; one error first grows, then both fall below 1% at 2,304 sites.

Our check: the paper’s bootstrap table reproduces exactly in rational arithmetic, with one error first growing, then both collapsing to below 1% at 2,304 sites.

3:12Where it stands

The preprints are unrefereed and unchanged since release. There is no Lean formalization, and OSVerify has not reviewed them. The authors’ verification programs ran and passed on our machine. Tasaki has revised an earlier paper to build on the second preprint, noting that no logical gap has been found so far in the portions examined, but that the verification is not yet complete. If they hold, Haldane’s prediction is now a theorem for the spin-one Heisenberg chain on even rings: spin one really does open a gap, even if the proven gap is at least 100 times smaller than the real one.
