The claim, in plain words

Take N spins, each up or down, and join random pairs of them by bonds with random signs: a plus bond wants its two spins to agree, a minus bond wants them to disagree. This is the Viana–Bray spin glass. Around a loop with an odd number of minus bonds, some bond must break. In our small example, a frustrated triangle plus one free spin, 12 of the 16 configurations tie for best. With many long loops, at low temperature the system can have many competing states.

The quantity of interest is the log of the partition function per spin as the system grows (the free energy is its negative divided by the inverse temperature). Mézard and Parisi’s cavity picture (2001, for a fixed-degree version) describes it through a hierarchy: a distribution of local fields, distributions of those distributions, and so on. Extending Franz and Leone, Panchenko and Talagrand proved in 2004 that every finite hierarchy gives an upper bound, and hoped the best of them is exact.

Result 221 is one OpenAI preprint, 36 pages, dated September 23, 2026. It claims that equality for Panchenko and Talagrand’s class: interactions on an even number of spins, with a factorized form and a sign condition, a Poisson number of interactions whose spins are chosen uniformly with replacement, and only first moments of the couplings and fields. At every temperature and density, the limit exists and equals the infimum of the hierarchical trial functional over all finite depths. This covers the Viana–Bray model, diluted even-spin models with symmetric couplings, soft random SAT with an even number of literals per clause, and their zero-temperature ground-state limits. Only the lower bound is new.

What it doesn’t claim

An odd number of spins per interaction (so not random 3-SAT, which a companion paper treats with a different functional), fixed-degree graphs, which depth is needed, a full-replica-symmetry-breaking functional, or a description of the actual Gibbs states.

How the proof goes

  1. The upper bound (old). Interpolate between the real system and one where each spin sees independent random messages. The error contains xp − p x yp−1 + (p−1) yp, nonnegative for even p by convexity; with the sign condition, every finite hierarchy bounds the limit from above. For p = 3 with signed factors it factors as (x − y)²(x + 2y), which can be negative.
  2. The lower bound (new). Build finite artificial hierarchies, let the system grow at each fixed depth, and only then deepen. Small random perturbations give identities for how replicas branch.
  3. The key step. Move every branching depth one level later; induction then makes every multioverlap concentrate on average over branching depths. A randomly chosen site’s hierarchy of messages, copied independently, becomes a trial whose value nearly matches the true increment, so the liminf is at least the infimum.

Extending Franz and Leone, in 2004 Panchenko and Talagrand proved that every finite hierarchy bounds that log from above, and hoped the best of them is exact.

Result 221 is one OpenAI preprint, 36 pages, dated September 23, 2026. It claims that equality, a conjecture since then, for Panchenko and Talagrand’s class of models: interactions joining an even number of spins, with a factorized form and a sign condition. 2:32How the proof goes

The upper bound interpolates between the real system and one where each spin sees independent random messages. The error contains x^p − p·x·y^(p−1) + (p−1)·y^p. For even p, convexity makes this nonnegative, and with the sign condition the error has the right sign. For p = 3 with signed factors, as in the three-spin model, it factors as (x − y)²(x + 2y), which can be negative. The new half is the lower bound. Rather than proving that the real states form a hierarchy, the paper builds finite artificial hierarchies, lets the system grow at each fixed depth, and only then deepens. Small random perturbations give identities for how replicas branch. The key step, also described in a reasoning summary OpenAI released, moves every branching depth one level later; induction then makes every multioverlap, an average of spin products across replicas, concentrate on average over branching depths. A randomly chosen site’s hierarchy of messages, copied independently, becomes a trial whose value nearly matches the true increment, so the liminf is at least the infimum. As an illustration, our computer summed over every configuration of small Viana–Bray samples, with mean degree three, up to twenty spins. Every average stayed below the simplest, paramagnetic, trial value, as the upper bound requires. Beyond inverse temperature about 0.66, population dynamics finds a better replica-symmetric trial: 1.901 against 1.976 at inverse temperature 1.5. Twenty spins can’t tell which depth is needed.

4:22Where it stands

The preprint is unrefereed and has not been revised. A Lean formalization proves the main theorem, not the corollaries: about 56,000 lines, with no unproved steps; we did not build it. Almost all of its files are reused by the release’s random 3-SAT result. The reasoning summary names three inspirations the paper does not cite. It is not among those OSVerify reviewed, and we found no expert reaction or earlier proof. If it holds, the physicists’ hierarchy of field distributions gives the exact answer for these diluted spin glasses at every temperature, settling the equality Panchenko and Talagrand hoped for in 2004.
