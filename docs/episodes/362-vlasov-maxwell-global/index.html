Plasmas never blow up?

An AI preprint claims global smoothness for the 3D relativistic Vlasov–Maxwell system

The claim, in plain words

A plasma is a gas of charged particles moving in the electric and magnetic fields they create. The relativistic Vlasov–Maxwell system describes it: a density of particles in position and momentum, carried along by the fields, and Maxwell’s equations driven by the particles. No particle can outrun light: our computer checks that the speed stays below one, approaching it as momentum grows. The worry is that some particles might reach ever higher momenta in finite time, so that a smooth solution breaks down.

Glassey and Strauss showed in 1986 that smooth solutions continue as long as the momenta stay bounded. Global weak solutions came from DiPerna and Lions, and from Rein for this relativistic system, and global smooth solutions were known for small data, for reduced dimensions and, from Wang’s recent work, for cylindrically symmetric data. For general large data in three dimensions, Luk and Strain called it a longstanding open problem in 2014, and a paper on several species of particles, posted the day after this one’s date, still called the large-data problem a crucial unsolved problem in the field.

Result 362 is one OpenAI paper, 49 pages, dated September 23 and made public October 6, 2026. It claims that for one species of particles in three dimensions, any smooth, nonnegative, compactly supported particle density with finite-energy fields whose derivatives are all bounded, and which satisfy Gauss’s laws, gives a unique classical solution that stays smooth on every finite time interval, and so exists for all time. There is no smallness and no symmetry assumption.

What it doesn’t claim

Not claimed: long-time behaviour, since the momenta may still grow with time; several species; or particle densities without compact support. Wang’s symmetric results allow different data, so neither result contains the other. The problem statement itself was given to the model in its prompt, according to OpenAI’s published summary. We found no competing claim.

How the proof goes

  1. Looking back along the light cone. The proof writes the fields as integrals over the backward light cone, controlled by the energy flowing through cones. Direct force bounds lose a square root of the receiving particle’s energy. The paper wins it back with an exact identity that integrates the force along the source particle’s path before taking sizes, a count of direction changes, and a careful selection of which bins need this treatment.
  2. Doublings take too long to blow up. The result bounds how fast the top momentum can grow: each doubling takes at least a constant divided by the logarithm of the momentum, roughly a constant over n for the n-th doubling. Like the harmonic series, those times never add up to a finite total: our computer’s sum reaches 16.5 after a million doublings and keeps growing, while a blow-up would need times with a finite total, such as times that halve at every step. So momenta stay bounded on every finite interval, and the Glassey–Strauss criterion gives global smoothness.
