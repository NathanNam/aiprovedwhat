Close means a small turn, proved at last?

Three AI preprints claim strong Kadison–Kastler stability for von Neumann algebras, and mark its limits

The claim, in plain words

Two algebras of operators on the same Hilbert space can be compared by how far apart their unit balls are, a distance Kadison and Kastler introduced in 1972. Rotating an algebra slightly moves it only slightly: rotate the diagonal 2×2 matrices by an angle, and the distance grows like the sine of twice the angle, reaching 1 at 45 degrees, while for small angles the best rotation back is about half that distance. Kadison and Kastler proposed that close von Neumann algebras must be a small rotation apart, conjugate by a unitary near the identity. In the late 1970s, Christensen and others proved this for amenable algebras, by averaging and then taking the polar part. Averaging needs amenability, so the general case stayed open; in 2012, Cameron, Christensen and coauthors gave the first non-amenable examples and stated the conjecture in its standard uniform form, one tolerance for all algebras.

Result 289 is three OpenAI preprints, 89 pages, dated September 23 and October 5, 2026. The first claims that conjecture for all von Neumann algebras: for every ε there is a δ, the same for every pair of unital von Neumann algebras on any Hilbert space, such that algebras closer than δ are conjugate by a unitary within ε of the identity. The other two papers mark its limits. If one algebra only nearly sits inside another, it can embed, yet every embedding unitary may stay a fixed distance from the identity. And arbitrarily close separable C*-algebras, with the same von Neumann closure, need not be conjugate by any unitary at all.

What it doesn’t claim

An explicit tolerance, a bound linear in the distance, or a C*-algebra version.

How the proof goes

  1. One tolerance for every algebra. For finite factors, the proof places the algebras in a common position and builds nearby unitaries indexed by a group that need not be amenable, then corrects them by averaging over an amenable action on a boundary, using double ergodicity. Matrix-level commutators are controlled with a constant of 600, proved independently of the companion similarity paper. Infinite factors reduce to finite corners through modular theory.
  2. Engines of the counterexamples. Clock and shift matrices commute ever more nearly as they grow, while a winding number stays at 1, so any path to a commuting pair must push the commutator up to 2. Sums of free unitaries stay small, at most about twice the square root of their number, and that lets a slightly tilted copy be detected.
