The claim, in plain words

A spin glass is a collection of magnets, each up or down, coupled by a matrix of interactions. In the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model the couplings are independent Gaussian numbers, and Parisi’s famous formula, proved by Guerra and Talagrand, gives its free energy. Here the couplings are built differently: choose the eigenvalues, almost any list you like, and rotate the eigenvectors uniformly at random. Semicircle eigenvalues give the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model; ±1 gives the random orthogonal model of Marinari, Parisi and Ritort. Does the pressure (the log of the average Boltzmann weight per spin) have a limit that depends only on the eigenvalue distribution, at every temperature? Rigorous answers existed only at high temperature (Bhattacharya and Sen; Fan and Wu; Fan, Misiakiewicz, Wang and Wen in 2026, whose explicit condition at the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick scale reaches a quarter of the way to the transition).

Result 234 is one OpenAI preprint, 59 pages, dated September 25, 2026. It claims an exact variational formula for the limiting pressure at every fixed temperature, on average and almost surely, whenever the eigenvalue distribution converges to a compactly supported law and the extreme eigenvalues converge to the edges of its support. The formula is an infimum over Parisi’s overlap profiles, and the spectrum enters only through an edge-extended version of its R-transform from free probability. It adds external fields, random spectra, the ground-state energy as the temperature goes to zero, and the existence of limits for two problems about Gaussian matrices; and it shows compact support matters: with an unbounded spectral law, one enormous eigenvalue can destroy the limit.

What it doesn’t claim

Numerical values, or a separate formula at zero temperature (the ground state is the limit of the formula).

How the proof goes

  1. Rotational invariance. The couplings are not Gaussian, so the usual interpolation does not apply; what survives is rotational invariance. First, small perturbations make the overlaps ultrametric. Then rotating a little between two eigenspaces gives exact identities, which show that the overlap of two copies inside each eigenspace is fixed by the total overlap alone.
  2. Bounds. An upper bound by a new minimality argument with Parisi-style trial fields, and a cavity lower bound with a new way of adding spins that keeps the eigenvalue proportions intact.

The numbers in the video

Here is the smallest case our computer worked out: four spins, eigenvalues plus one twice and minus one twice, one random rotation. 2:42How the proof goes

For the Gaussian model, interpolation arguments work directly. Here the couplings are not Gaussian; what survives is rotational invariance. First, small perturbations make the overlaps ultrametric. Then rotating a little between two eigenspaces gives exact identities, which show that the overlap of two copies inside each eigenspace is fixed by the total overlap alone. Then come an upper bound, by a new minimality argument with Parisi-style trial fields, and a cavity lower bound, with a new way of adding spins that keeps the eigenvalue proportions intact. As a consistency check, our computer fed the paper’s formula the semicircle law and compared it with Parisi’s formula for the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model, on one-step trial profiles at three temperatures. They agree to 6 decimal places, which the paper does not state. Our exact enumeration of all spin states, up to 22 spins and 14,200 samples, extrapolates to the high-temperature value, though for the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick spectrum at low temperature such small systems are far from the limit.
