Stable blowup for waves that spread?

An AI preprint claims an open set of finite-time blowups for a defocusing Schrödinger equation on the 12-dimensional torus

The claim, in plain words

The nonlinear Schrödinger equation describes waves that interact with themselves. In the defocusing version the interaction pushes the wave apart, so the natural expectation is that solutions spread out and stay smooth forever. Our computer runs a one-dimensional toy: a defocusing pulse falls from height two to about 0.56, while the focusing pulse holds together. Later papers report that Bourgain conjectured in 2000 that, even for powers above the energy-critical level, defocusing solutions exist for all time and spread out.

In 2019, Merle, Raphaël, Rodnianski and Szeftel showed otherwise: in certain high dimensions and powers, solutions built from imploding compressible fluids blow up in finite time. Cao-Labora, Gómez-Serrano, Shi and Staffilani extended this to non-radial and periodic settings in 2024. But those blowups came from a thin family of starting data, and the first group wrote that counting the unstable directions remained to be addressed. Could blowup happen for a whole open set of data?

Result 371 is one OpenAI paper, 56 pages, dated September 24 and made public October 6, 2026. It claims that on the twelve-dimensional torus, for some odd power that can be taken as large as you like, a nonempty open set of starting data with many derivatives blows up in finite time. The blowup is self-similar: the spike narrows like the square root of the remaining time while its height grows. At power 101, our computer finds the height grows only about ten percent while the width shrinks a hundredfold. A corollary: random starting data, as smooth as you like, blow up with positive probability, giving a negative answer to a version, not the exact wording, of an open problem of Deng, Nahmod and Yue.

What it doesn’t claim

It was not the first self-similar blowup of this kind. On September 20, four days before the paper’s printed date, Buck, Carballeira, Gómez-Serrano and Shi posted one in three, four and five dimensions, for a thin family of data, and disclosed using OpenAI models. The OpenAI paper cites it and says earlier constructions are not inputs to its proofs. What is new here is the open set, the first self-similar blowup of this kind on a torus, and the random-data corollary. Not claimed: a specific power, every large power, or blowup with probability one.

How the proof goes

  1. The spike’s shape, and its few unstable directions. As the power grows, the spike’s profile approaches a flat core of radius about 3.3, glued to an outer part given by a classical special function. Exact rational certificates show the two pieces can be glued, with a certified mismatch below 2.5 × 10⁻¹¹ at the printed matching point. The same certificates count the modes that fail to decay in that limit: two, one and zero for angular degrees zero, one and higher, and degree one comes in twelve directions. So, after a long analysis at large finite powers, the only directions that do not decay are the fourteen symmetries: one phase, twelve translations and one blowup time.
  2. From decay to an open set. Everything else decays, first in all of space and then on ever larger periodic boxes. A nonlinear stable graph, plus shifting the blowup point, time and phase, plus a topological degree argument, then fills an open set of data. Random Gaussian data give every open set positive probability, and our computer illustrates that the paper’s condition on alpha is exactly the threshold for that random data to have finite size in twelve dimensions.
