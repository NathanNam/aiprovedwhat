The claim, in plain words

A perceptron is the simplest artificial neuron: N weights and a list of random patterns, each of which must have a positive dot product with the weights. How many patterns can one set of weights satisfy? For weights on a sphere, two per weight: by Cover’s count, the chance of success at that density is exactly one half for every N. For weights that are only ±1, Krauth and Mézard predicted about 0.833 per weight in 1989. In a tiny case, three weights and three patterns, only one of the eight sign choices works; our exhaustive search up to 22 weights falls from 0.92 toward 0.85. Physicists also study a softer version, where each violated pattern costs a factor e−β, and ask for the pressure: the log of the average Boltzmann factor, per weight.

Result 222 is four OpenAI preprints, 248 pages, dated September 24, 2026. Two claim that pressure exactly, at every density and every positive temperature: for ±1 weights with any bounded reward, and for spherical weights with any bounded continuous reward. Each answer is an infimum over a whole overlap profile, a Parisi-type formula with full replica symmetry breaking; for spherical weights, Györgyi and Reimann predicted it in 2000. A third paper treats patterns from bi-orthogonally invariant random matrices.

The fourth paper, 133 pages, goes to zero temperature in one model: spherical weights, margin −1, and a quadratic penalty. Taking the system size to infinity, then the temperature to zero, then the density down to its critical value from the overcrowded side, it claims a sharp jamming threshold (with no value), one contact per degree of freedom, and cumulative power laws for small gaps and forces with exponents 0.4126930 < γ < 0.4126934 and 0.4231063 < θ < 0.4231088. Physicists predicted these values for hard spheres in 2014 and for this perceptron in 2016.

What it doesn’t claim

The hard capacity itself: the Ising paper says the hard-constraint limit needs extra control of the order of limits. A July 2026 preprint by Shmalo, which says most of its work was done by AI systems, claims to close the remaining gaps in the 0.833 proof by computer. Nor are values of the free energies or of the jamming threshold given. Our evaluation of the Ising paper’s functional at its simplest trial, at zero temperature and outside the theorem, gives a solution entropy that crosses zero at 0.83308.

How the proof goes

  1. Upper bound. The free-energy proofs adapt the spin-glass proofs of the Parisi formula. A general reward gives no sign for the usual interpolation, so they use Mourrat’s method: enrich the system with a hierarchy of Gaussian fields, treat the number of patterns as time, and show that if the bound failed, a comparison would have a negative minimum, where overlap identities and Panchenko’s ultrametricity force a contradiction.
  2. Lower bound. Remove two patterns, restore them as independent marks, and add a few weights at a time: the cavity method.
  3. Jamming. The jamming paper proves its own pressure formula for the unbounded penalty, takes the limits in order, and shows the critical profile scales as a power law near jamming. The exponents come from a number a* where an eigenvalue equals its own parameter: γ = 1 − 2a*, and γ = 1/(2 + θ) follows. a* is pinned down by a 681-line Python certificate printed in the paper; our run matches the printed hash and reaches the same 24 checkpoints, which shows the code runs, not that its error analysis is right.

The numbers in the video

  • Ours: Cover’s count; exhaustive enumeration of small Ising perceptrons; the Ising functional at constant paths (0.83308, outside the theorem).
  • Ours: gradient-descent minima of the margin −1 model near jamming, up to 800 weights: 1.019, 1.016 and 1.005 contacts per weight; the gap and force slopes have not settled. Local minima at finite size, not the paper’s limit. The Lean line count is ours.

3:11How the proof goes

The free-energy proofs adapt the spin-glass proofs of the Parisi formula, with a twist. A general reward gives no sign for the usual interpolation, so the upper bound uses Mourrat’s method: enrich the system with a hierarchy of Gaussian fields, treat the number of patterns as time, and show that if the bound failed, a comparison would have a negative minimum, where overlap identities and Panchenko’s ultrametricity force a contradiction. The jamming paper proves its own pressure formula for the unbounded penalty, takes the limits in order, and shows the critical profile scales as a power law near jamming. The exponents come from a number a* where an eigenvalue equals its own parameter: γ = 1 − 2a*, and γ = 1/(2 + θ) follows. a* is pinned down by a 681-line Python certificate printed in the paper. Our computer extracted it, matched the paper’s printed hash, and ran it to the same 24 checkpoints. That shows the code runs, not that its error analysis is right. As an illustration, our computer minimized the penalty for up to 800 weights, deleting patterns until the system just jams. Contacts per weight fell from 1.02 to 1.005, close to one. The gap and force curves look roughly like power laws, but at these sizes their slopes have not settled at the predicted values. These are local minima at finite size, not the paper’s limit.

4:49Where it stands

All four preprints are unrefereed and unrevised. In Lean, the spherical formula is formalized in full, about 71,000 lines; for the Ising paper, only that the formula is finite, for continuous rewards; the structured paper not at all, and the jamming paper only in unlisted supporting lemmas; we did not build any of it. The relation between the two exponents got a physics-level proof in June 2026 by Parisi and Zamponi, which the jamming paper credits. None is among those OSVerify reviewed, and we found no expert reaction or earlier proof. If they hold, the physicists’ replica formulas for the perceptron are exact at every positive temperature, and the jamming exponents of the margin-minus-one perceptron are theorems, pinned to within a few millionths.
