The claims, in plain words

Take a contraction f: X → Z with connected fibers, where minus the canonical divisor K is ample over Z: a Fano contraction. A complement near a point of Z is an effective boundary B that makes K + B trivial there: N(K + B) is an integral Cartier divisor linearly equivalent to zero, and N is the index. On the projective line over a point, two points with coefficient one give index one, but that pair is only log canonical; a klt complement needs every coefficient below one, like four points with coefficient one half, index two.

Shokurov introduced complements in his work on threefold flips and conjectured that their index can be bounded in each dimension. Birkar proved bounded log canonical complements, a key step toward his boundedness theorem for Fano varieties. Klt complements are harder, because bad singularities push the index up: the cone over a rational normal curve of degree n has a vertex with log discrepancy 2/n, and its klt complements need index at least n/2. So one fixes ε and allows only ε-lc varieties, whose log discrepancies are all at least ε. Chen reduced the conjecture to a local threshold statement and proved it over bases of dimension at most two.

Result 066 is two OpenAI preprints, both dated September 25, 2026. The first (93 pages) claims: for each dimension d and rational ε there is an index N(d, ε) such that every ε-lc Fano contraction of dimension d, over any algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, has a klt complement of that index near every point of the base. Over ℂ this gives the finite-rational-coefficient form of Shokurov’s bounded-klt-complement conjecture, as Chen stated it. The second (106 pages) claims the Cartier-divisor conjecture of Birkar and Shokurov: for an ε-lc Fano type contraction with nef −(K + B) and a positive-dimensional base, through any base point there is a Cartier divisor whose pullback has log canonical threshold bounded below in terms of the dimension and ε alone. Over ℂ, Chen showed the two conjectures are equivalent; the papers reach them by independent routes.

What they don’t claim

The complements are klt, not ε-lc (the stronger version in Chen’s Remark 1.2 is not asserted), and the coefficient version needs a finite set of rational coefficients. The index depends on the dimension and ε; without a fixed ε it cannot be bounded, as the cones show.

How the proof goes

  1. A threshold. Through Chen’s equivalence, the first paper needs a uniform lower bound for the log canonical threshold of the pullback of a base point’s maximal ideal, in the birational case with no boundary. For the cones this threshold is exactly 2/n, never below ε for ε-lc members.
  2. By contradiction. A sequence breaking the bound has divisors whose order along the ideal is huge compared with their log discrepancy. Small log discrepancy at a real valuation is not enough, because rescaling rescales it, so the proof must also control the normalization.
  3. Volumes and degenerations. Anticanonical sections are encoded in an affine cone; normalized volume is minimized under a log canonical constraint, after an exact-penalization step; stable degeneration and a torus of maximal rank give a graded model, and counts of independent sections control the valuations.
  4. Traces. An auxiliary reduction to positive characteristic uses Cartier trace maps, compared with multiplier ideals through Takagi’s theorem, to produce sections of controlled degree and value. Values are rounded to integers layer by layer until the initial terms form a full transcendence basis; then a bounded multiple of the valuation is a whole-number multiple of a prime divisor whose log discrepancy is below ε, a contradiction.
  5. The companion route. The Cartier paper uses characters on affine cones and a pinning obstruction instead.

2:33A threshold, by contradiction

Through Chen’s equivalence, the first paper needs a uniform lower bound for one number: the log canonical threshold of the pullback of the maximal ideal of a base point, in the birational case with no boundary. For the cones, that threshold is exactly 2/n, the same as the log discrepancy, so it is never below ε. The paper argues by contradiction. A sequence breaking the bound has divisors whose order along the ideal is huge compared with their log discrepancy. The goal is a prime divisor with log discrepancy below ε, which ε-lc forbids. Small log discrepancy at a real valuation is not enough, because rescaling a valuation rescales it; the proof must also control how the divisor is normalized. It encodes anticanonical sections in an affine cone and minimizes normalized volume, using work of Li, Blum, Xu and Zhuang, under a log canonical constraint, after showing that a small perturbation makes the constrained minimizer an ordinary one. Stable degeneration and a torus of maximal rank give a graded model, and counts of independent sections control the possible valuations.

3:49Traces and the companion route

The last stage borrows a tool from positive characteristic. Cartier trace maps, compared with multiplier ideals through Takagi’s theorem, turn a strict budget for the valuation into actual sections of controlled degree and value. Values are rounded to integers, layer by layer, until the initial terms form a full transcendence basis. Then a bounded multiple of the valuation is a whole-number multiple of a prime divisor, and the final choice of constants makes its log discrepancy less than ε: the contradiction. The companion paper reaches the Cartier statement differently, with characters on cones and a pinning obstruction. It adapts two lemmas from one of the release papers of episode 037, without using its theorem as a black box. OpenAI’s Lean check concerns one supporting lemma of that companion paper: a compactness statement for normalized chart-field retractions. It does not cover either main theorem.

4:50Where it stands

Neither preprint is peer reviewed yet, and neither has been revised. The only Lean check is that supporting lemma. Their inputs include Birkar’s boundedness theorems and complements, Chen’s reduction, minimal model theory, Ambro’s canonical bundle formula, normalized volumes, and Takagi’s comparison of multiplier and test ideals. Both papers are long, and each adds new technical steps that experts must check. We found no public assessment. If they hold, Shokurov’s program gets a central piece: in each dimension, once the singularities are uniformly mild, Fano contractions have klt complements of bounded index, and every base point lies on a local Cartier divisor whose pullback has uniformly controlled singularities.
