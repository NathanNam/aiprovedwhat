One irrep, not compact?

The claim, in plain words

The compact operators are the infinite matrices that are almost finite, and up to a change of basis they act irreducibly in only one way. In 1951 Naimark asked the converse: is a C*-algebra with only one irreducible representation, up to equivalence, always the compact operators? Rosenberg answered yes in 1953 when that representation acts on a separable Hilbert space, which covers every separable algebra, so any counterexample has to be nonseparable. In 2004 Akemann and Weaver built a counterexample assuming Jensen’s diamond principle, an extra axiom independent of ZFC, and whether ZFC alone gives one stayed open.

On September 22, 2026, a preprint by Ryotaro Tanaka gave a ZFC counterexample; its AI-use statement says the construction and proofs were developed predominantly by OpenAI’s ChatGPT, supervised and reviewed by the author. Result 297 is one OpenAI paper, 23 pages, dated September 24, 2026, and published October 6. It credits Tanaka and gives an alternative proof: in ZFC, a unital infinite-dimensional simple C*-algebra with a faithful tracial state, whose nonzero irreducible representations are all equivalent, so it is not the compact operators.

What it doesn’t claim

Tanaka’s theorem says more: a unique trace, a faithful representation on a separable Hilbert space, and a precise size. What the release adds is a different method; it claims no exact size for its algebra, only an upper bound.

The main theorem (docs)

Last checked October 18, 2026. The paper has not been revised. In Lean, the main theorem is formalized and matches the paper, in about 26,000 lines, with no unproved steps in our scan (we did not build it). Lean’s logic is somewhat stronger than ZFC, a point raised on the Lean Zulip and relayed on MathOverflow; Nik Weaver, having read the manuscript, did not think it an issue. OSVerify has not reviewed it. On MathOverflow, Weaver wrote that it was “probably fair to say that the conventional wisdom was that a positive solution would also be consistent with ZFC.”

How the proof goes

  1. Gluing, one pair at a time. The construction starts from the CAR algebra and adjoins unitaries one at a time by reduced HNN extensions. Each new unitary merges exactly one chosen pair of classes of pure states, and no other pair of old classes.
  2. A schedule instead of a guess. Each stage can hold continuum many pure states, and gluing can create new ones, so the gluing runs for the successor of the continuum many steps, on a fixed schedule that never treats a state before it exists. Every pure state is determined on a separable piece, so the schedule reaches it and joins it to one reference class; that replaces the guessing of the diamond principle.

The construction starts from the CAR algebra and adjoins unitaries one at a time by reduced HNN extensions. Each new unitary merges exactly one chosen pair of classes of pure states, and no other pair of old classes. Our computer shows the finite version: gluing six 2×2 blocks with five unitaries takes six classes down to one, ending at the 12×12 matrices, merging exactly the chosen pair each time. In the infinite case each stage can hold continuum many pure states, and gluing can create new ones, so the gluing runs for the successor of the continuum many steps, on a fixed schedule that never treats a state before it exists. Our computer checks such a schedule on a 60×60 grid.
