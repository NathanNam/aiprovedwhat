One trap for every start?

The claim, in plain words

A chemical reaction network is a list of reactions, such as 2A ⇄ B. Under mass action, each reaction fires at a rate proportional to the product of its input concentrations, with a fixed rate constant, so the concentrations follow a polynomial differential equation. Conservation laws confine each trajectory to its stoichiometric compatibility class (here a + 2b never changes), and a class can be unbounded. So a concentration could blow up, or sink toward zero. Weak reversibility is a condition on the reaction graph, whose nodes are complexes like 2A: whenever one complex can turn into another, some chain of reactions leads back. Without it things can fail: in our run of A → 0 with 2A → 3A, a start at 1/2 dies out and a start at 2 blows up at time ln 2, while the weakly reversible completion brings every start from 10−9 to 109 within a factor of two of 1 after about 0.64 time units. Even with weak reversibility recovery can be slow: in A ⇄ 2A, a start at 10−12 takes about 27.6 time units to reach 1/2.

As the papers recount, the theory begins with Horn and Jackson and with Feinberg; for complex-balanced systems it gives a unique positive equilibrium in each class and an entropy-like Lyapunov function. Feinberg raised the question of excluding limits with a zero concentration, and Anderson separated the boundedness conjecture from the persistence conjecture. Permanence asks for one compact set per class, kept away from zero, that eventually holds every trajectory. As the papers describe them, Gopalkrishnan, Miller and Shiu proved permanence for strongly endotactic networks, which include every weakly reversible network with one linkage class; Boros and Hofbauer gave another single-linkage proof; Craciun, Nazarov and Pantea treated two species; Pantea proved persistence of bounded trajectories when the reaction directions span a plane; and Craciun’s revised manuscript of September 23, 2026 gives persistence for bounded trajectories. Those results also allow rates that vary in time within fixed positive bounds.

Result 149 is two OpenAI preprints. The first (16 pages, September 25, 2026) claims, in its Theorem 1.1, that for every finite weakly reversible network with fixed positive rates, every positive start gives a solution that exists for all time and stays between ε and 1/ε, with ε depending on the network, the rates and the start: boundedness and persistence, whether or not the class is bounded. It notes that August and Barahona stated this in 2010, and argues that a step of their proof fails. The second (13 pages, October 5, 2026) is the headline: its Theorem 1.1 says each positive class, even an unbounded one, has a single compact convex forward-invariant set that every trajectory in the class enters in finite time, so after a transient all trajectories in the class share the same bounds. “Thus the fixed-positive-rate, weakly reversible formulation of the permanence conjecture holds, including on unbounded classes.”

What it doesn’t claim

The entry time depends on the start, and the trap depends on the class and the rates. In our example 0 ⇄ X + Y, the equilibrium of each class has its smaller concentration near one over the gap x − y once that gap is large, so no single box serves every class. Nothing is claimed about converging to an equilibrium, except under complex balance (the first paper’s Remark 3.5, which it calls “the known global-attractor conclusion”): in our run of a weakly reversible network, all 24 of our starts enter one box and then circle a cycle of period about 20. Rates are fixed in time, the bounds are not explicit, and the papers call these conjectures but never call them famous or long-standing.

OpenAI published a Lean formalization of the first paper’s theorem (global existence, and bounds between ε and 1/ε depending on the start): a comparator statement with the network, weak reversibility and the mass-action equation written out, and 71 files and 7,758 lines of proof, with no unproved steps that we could find. We have not run the check. The second paper’s classwise permanence, the headline claim, has no Lean formalization anywhere in the repository. Neither paper is among those OSVerify reviewed, and we have seen no expert review.

How the proof goes

  1. Levels and cuts. Pick a tiny scale h and write each concentration as a power of h. Each complex’s monomial is then h raised to a power, its level, and since h is tiny the complex at the lowest level dominates. Cut between two levels: a reaction crossing the cut downward has a path back that must cross upward somewhere, from a vastly larger monomial, so one upward flux beats the whole downward flux and a linear level function cannot decrease. In our far-out example every cut has upward flux at least 1 and downward flux at most 0.001.
  2. A function that never decreases. Near a tie the comparison can flip, so both papers build a minimum of finitely many affine functions with slopes fixed in advance, whose active slopes track the point’s log coordinates so closely that clear comparisons keep their sign and near ties become exact ties (the same four-page lemma in both papers). The first paper picks h so small that the start looks central; the top plateau then contains the start and is a compact convex set inside the box, and since the function never decreases there, the trajectory never leaves it.
  3. One trap per class. The second paper fixes a class. An affine piece whose slope is perpendicular to the class is constant on it and never below the plateau value, so below the plateau every active slope has a reaction crossing a cut, and the function climbs at a definite rate; capped at the plateau, any trajectory that stays in the box reaches it in finite time. The plateau at scale h sits inside the smaller box for √h, so a start trapped at a tiny scale climbs a ladder of square roots up to one scale fixed for the class, whose plateau, cut down to the class, is the common trap.

The numbers in the video

  • A ⇄ 2A (rates 1): from a0 the time to reach 1/2 is ln((1 − a0)/a0), 27.6 from 10−12.
  • A → 0 with 2A → 3A (rates 1): from 1/2, a(10) = 4.5 × 10−5; from 2, blow-up at t = ln 2. The completion 0 ⇄ A, 2A ⇄ 3A: every start from 10−9 to 109 is in [1/2, 2] by t ≈ 0.64.
  • 0 ⇄ X + Y (rates 1): the class x − y = Δ has smaller equilibrium concentration 0.618, 0.0990, 0.0100 and 0.0010 for Δ = 1, 10, 100 and 1000.
  • 0 ⇄ X (rates 0.05, 1), 0 ⇄ Y (0.5, 0.001), 2X + Y ⇄ 3X (1, 0.001): weakly reversible, deficiency one, not complex-balanced; a periodic orbit of period 19.7; 24 starts from 10−6 to 106 stay in [0.01, 10]2 from t ≤ 11.4. With two species, permanence here was already covered by Craciun, Nazarov and Pantea.
  • The network X + Y ⇄ 2Z, 0 → X → Y → Z → 0 at the far-out point with h = 10−30: upward cut fluxes 5 × 108, 5 × 108, 1, 1, 1; downward at most 10−3.
