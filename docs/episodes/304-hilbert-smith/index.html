The claim, in plain words

Hilbert’s fifth problem, from 1900, asked how far Lie’s theory of continuous symmetry survives without smoothness. Gleason, and Montgomery and Zippin, settled it in 1952 for groups that are themselves manifolds, and Yamabe extended the theory to all locally compact groups. The Hilbert–Smith conjecture, named for P. A. Smith, asks the same for groups that act on a manifold: a locally compact group acting faithfully on a connected manifold must be a Lie group. By classical work, it is enough to show that the p-adic integers cannot act faithfully. They form a group shaped like a Cantor set, with whole copies of themselves in every small ball, which Lie groups never have. The conjecture was known in dimensions up to 2, by Montgomery and Zippin, and in dimension 3, by Pardon in 2013, and for Lipschitz, Hölder and quasiconformal actions. Yang showed that a faithful action would make the orbit space jump from dimension n to n + 2, in cohomological dimension: strange, but not a contradiction.

Result 304 is one OpenAI paper, 46 pages, dated September 23, 2026, and made public October 6. It claims that the p-adic integers have no faithful, jointly continuous action on any connected, Hausdorff, second-countable manifold without boundary, in any finite dimension. With the classical reduction, this gives the Hilbert–Smith conjecture for second-countable groups in every finite dimension. The manifold may be noncompact, nonorientable or not triangulable.

What it doesn’t claim

Anything about infinite-dimensional manifolds, where the p-adic integers do act faithfully, on the Hilbert cube; groups or manifolds that are not second-countable; or homology manifolds. The reduction to the p-adic integers is cited, not reproved.

How the proof goes

  1. A signature with bounded denominators. The proof shrinks to a tiny subgroup acting on one chart, crosses with Euclidean space to reach an odd dimension d, and averages a degree-one pinch over the group to get a test map through the orbit space. It builds a new real signature invariant, from Witt groups of sheaves pushed forward along arbitrary continuous maps, and proves that on the odd sphere its integral classes have denominators bounded by a fixed power of 2.
  2. Pieces that cannot fit. Splitting the sphere’s signature class by characters of the group gives pᵏ integral pieces of equal size, summing to four times the orientation class, for every k. Each piece would be 4/pᵏ, which, once k is large enough, does not fit. The orbit space never needs a dimension bound, so the proof never goes through Yang’s dimension jump.
