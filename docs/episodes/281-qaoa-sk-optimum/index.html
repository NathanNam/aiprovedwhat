QAOA reaches the optimum, proved at last?

Two AI preprints claim QAOA attains the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick optimum, size first and depth second

The claim, in plain words

Picture a magnet in which every pair of spins is linked by a random coupling, some pulling them alike, some apart: the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick spin glass. As it grows, the best arrangement’s score per spin approaches a constant given by Parisi’s formula, about 0.763, called P*. The quantum approximate optimization algorithm, QAOA, alternates two kinds of pulses, p rounds of each, with tunable angles. For infinitely many spins its best score at depth 1 is 1/√(4e), about 0.303; published values reach 0.591 at depth 7 (our computer reproduces them) and about 0.748 at depth 160. In 2021 Basso, Farhi, Marwaha, Villalonga and Zhou conjectured that these values reach P* as the depth grows, with the number of spins going to infinity first.

Result 281 is two OpenAI preprints, 97 pages, dated September 25 and 27, 2026. The main paper claims that conjecture. For every accuracy, some finite depth and fixed angles, not depending on the size or the couplings, get that close on average as the size grows. A corollary: on random regular graphs of large degree, QAOA’s expected cut comes as close as desired to the best cut at leading order, 1/2 + P*/√degree.

What it doesn’t claim

Any bound on the depth, a recipe for the angles, guarantees at finite size, or a speedup. A classical algorithm by Montanari, unconditional since July 2026, already gets within any accuracy in time growing like the square of the size.

How the proof goes

  1. No gaps. Approximating Parisi’s solution with more and more steps gives upper bounds falling from 0.798 to 0.765, then 0.7636, toward P*. The companion paper proves that the full zero-temperature solution has no gaps, a result Hong-Bin Chen had posted in July, as the paper acknowledges. That is what lets message passing, the classical method of Montanari and colleagues, reach the optimum.
  2. Imitation. For a fixed circuit at infinite size, the spin glass scores the same as an infinitely bushy tree (an identity of Basso and colleagues), and the messages passed along the tree can be built from quantum pulses, first with temporary random seed fields, which echo constructions then remove. What remains is one fixed, finite sequence of pulses, possibly with very large angles.

The numbers in the video

  • Ours: QAOA’s infinite-size values for depth 1 to 7, 0.3033 to 0.5915, matching the published tables.
  • Published: 0.6773 at depth 17 (Basso and colleagues, 2021) and 0.7483 at depth 160 (Boulebnane and colleagues, September 30, 2026).
  • Ours: step-function upper bounds on the Parisi value, 0.798, 0.765, 0.7636, 0.7633 and 0.7632, against P* ≈ 0.763166.
