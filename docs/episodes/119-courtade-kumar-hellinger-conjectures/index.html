The best bit?

Claimed proofs of the Courtade–Kumar conjecture on the most informative Boolean function, and of the stronger Hellinger conjecture

The claims, in plain words

Flip n fair coins, then send them through a noisy channel that flips each one independently with probability ε. Before the noise, you may summarize the coins by a single yes-or-no answer, any Boolean function. Which summary shares the most information with the noisy observation? Kumar and Courtade conjectured in 2013 that the best is simply to report one coin, a dictator, which keeps 1 − h2(ε) bits, at every noise level and in every dimension. Majority, for instance, keeps less.

At high noise, Ordentlich, Shayevitz and Weinstein found nearly sharp bounds for balanced functions, and Samorodnitsky proved the conjecture for all functions in a dimension-independent high-noise range; Yu extended the range for balanced functions with computer assistance. In 2017 Anantharam, Bogdanov, Chakrabarti, Jayram and Nair formulated a Hellinger conjecture that implies Courtade–Kumar, and Chen, Gohari and Nair developed a differential approach. Several other recent preprints also announce proofs of the Courtade–Kumar conjecture.

Result 119 is two OpenAI preprints dated September 24, 2026. The first (29 pages) proves a sharp contraction for any randomized binary summary: at a fixed initial information, a noisy coordinate keeps the most. For Boolean functions this gives the Courtade–Kumar inequality, and a refined bound that accounts for the function’s bias; it also proves a mean-dependent entropy-production bound. The paper notes that a production bound in an earlier preprint by Chen and coauthors, integrated from the initial information, also gives its main theorem. The second (50 pages) proves the Hellinger conjecture for every output bias and noise level: for a Boolean function of mean m, √(1 − m²) − E√(1 − (Tρf)²) ≤ 1 − √(1 − ρ²), with ρ = 1 − 2ε and equality for signed coordinates. It implies the Courtade–Kumar bound again; its proof is computer-assisted through fixed scalar inequalities with exact rational certificates.

What it doesn’t claim

These are not the only claimed proofs of the Courtade–Kumar inequality: other groups have announced proofs by different routes, and the first paper itself notes that an earlier preprint’s production bound yields its main theorem. The Hellinger form is the distinct contribution. The refined bound for biased functions is an upper curve; the paper does not claim every biased function attains it.

How the proofs go

  1. Information along the noise. As the noise grows, information drops at a rate given by an entropy production. If that production is always at least what a soft coordinate with the same information would produce, the effective amplitude shrinks at least as fast as a coordinate’s, which is the theorem. Following Chen, Gohari and Nair, the production inequality is proved by induction on dimension: split the cube along one coordinate and make the joining edges pay for the increase.
  2. The joining problem. Tracking information alone fails for some pairs, so the proof keeps the mean as well, solving the local joining problem with an explicit three-point optimizer and a common entropy budget, two entropy capacities and a common-output thinning inequality.
  3. The Hellinger proof. With s = √(1 − ρ²): for s ≤ 0.84, compression to increasing functions and dimension induction with an asymmetric profile and a sheared two-point comparison; for larger s, very biased functions are handled directly, and otherwise, at a first failure, an energy estimate along the noise forces a signed coordinate. The scalar inequalities, independent of the dimension, are certified with exact arithmetic.

The numbers in the video

  • Every Boolean function on up to 4 bits (65,536 on 4 bits), at four noise levels: the bound held, and equality occurred exactly for the signed dictators; the refined and Hellinger bounds held too.
  • At ε = 1/4, the best other function on 4 bits keeps 0.161 bits against 0.189 for a dictator; on 7 bits, majority keeps 0.134 bits.
  • 3,000 random soft summaries on 5 bits never beat the sharp contraction curve.

2:58How the proofs go

The first proof works along the noise. As the noise grows, information drops at a rate given by an entropy production. If that production is always at least what a soft coordinate with the same information would produce, then the effective amplitude shrinks at least as fast as a coordinate’s, which is the theorem. Following Chen, Gohari and Nair, the production inequality is proved by induction on dimension: split the cube along one coordinate, and make the edges joining the two halves pay for the increase. The joining step cannot track information alone: Chen, Gohari and Nair showed that such a comparison fails for some pairs. The new proof keeps the mean as well. It solves the local joining problem with an explicit three-point optimizer and a common entropy budget, and uses two entropy capacities and a common-output thinning inequality to cover every case. The Hellinger proof splits by noise level. Write s for √(1 − ρ²). For s at most 0.84, it compresses to increasing functions and inducts on dimension, with an asymmetric profile and a sheared two-point comparison. For larger s, it continues from that range: very biased functions are handled directly, and otherwise, at a first failure, an energy estimate along the noise would force the function to be a signed coordinate. The remaining scalar inequalities, independent of the dimension, are certified with exact arithmetic. In our check, the bound held for every Boolean function on up to 4 bits at 4 noise levels, with equality only for the dictators. At ε = 1/4, the best other function on 4 bits kept 0.16 bits, against 0.19 for a dictator, and majority on 7 bits kept 0.13. The Hellinger inequality held with the same maximizers, and 3,000 random soft summaries on 5 bits never beat the sharp contraction curve.

5:00Where it stands

The preprints are not peer reviewed and have not been revised. The Lean formalization covers the sharp contraction for soft summaries, the Courtade–Kumar inequality with equality for dictators, the bias refinement and the entropy-production bound; the Hellinger paper is not formalized. We have not run the check. Neither paper is among those OSVerify reviewed, and other groups have announced proofs of the Courtade–Kumar conjecture. If they hold, the most informative Boolean function under bit-flip noise is a single coordinate, at every noise level and in every dimension. Alongside the other recent proofs, that confirms a conjecture open since 2013, and these preprints also prove its stronger Hellinger form, which the others do not claim.
