A noisy qubit’s capacity, proved at last?

An AI preprint claims the classical capacity of generalized amplitude damping

The claim, in plain words

Send bits through a noisy quantum channel. A qubit stored in its excited state tends to decay, and at a finite temperature it also gets kicked up: generalized amplitude damping, described by a damping strength γ and a thermal population ν. The sphere of qubit states shrinks to an ellipsoid and drifts toward the thermal state.

How many classical bits per use can it carry? Holevo, Schumacher and Westmoreland showed the answer is a limit over many uses, with inputs that may be entangled across uses, and Hastings showed in 2009 that such entangled inputs can help for some channels. For a single use, the best rate was already known as a formula; whether entangled inputs could beat it was open. A 2018 paper called the classical capacity of even plain amplitude damping a major open problem. On September 23, 2026, Tang, Zhu, Bai and Wang settled the plain amplitude-damping case, with help from AI models.

Result 276 is one OpenAI preprint, 20 pages, dated September 24, 2026. It claims the classical capacity of every generalized amplitude-damping channel: entangled inputs never help, so the capacity is the one-use formula, an optimization over a single variable, attained by pairs of equally likely signal states used independently and decoded jointly. It also claims additivity when combined with any finite-dimensional channel.

What it doesn’t claim

The quantum or private capacity, other channels, or a closed formula beyond special cases. And the paper was not alone: on October 6, the day of the release, Kun Fang posted an independent proof of the same result, by a related route through Rényi entropies.

How the proof goes

  1. A thermal-block entropy inequality. The key tool is a new entropy inequality for thermal blocks, extending one that Tang and coauthors used at zero temperature. It bounds the output entropy of many uses from below while tracking excitation populations, which is what keeps the bound tight; a cruder bound that ignores them overshoots the capacity by up to almost 0.6 bits (our computation).

The numbers in the video

  • Ours: the formula matches a brute-force optimization to 10 decimal places; with damping 0.3 and thermal population 0.2 the capacity is 0.61 bits per use; at ν = 1/2 it is King’s formula.
  • Ours: over two uses, the best entangled inputs we found do no better than two separate uses.
  • Ours: the capacity over all damping strengths and thermal populations, as a heat map.
