Every free group, one factor, proved at last?

The claim, in plain words

A free group on n letters is the group of all words in those letters and their inverses, with no relations at all; in two letters there are 4 reduced words of length 1, 12 of length 2, 36 of length 3, tripling each time. The group’s von Neumann algebra L(Fn) is a factor for two or more letters, and in the 1940s Murray and von Neumann showed it differs from the simplest, hyperfinite factor. But can L(F2) be told apart from L(F3)? Kadison listed the question in 1967. In 1994, building on Voiculescu’s free probability, Dykema and Rădulescu, independently, showed a dichotomy: the free group factors are either all isomorphic or all different. Most experts expected different. By Dykema’s formula, L(Fr) stretched by t is L(F1+(r−1)/t²).

Result 287 is one OpenAI preprint, 23 pages, dated September 23, 2026. It claims the answer is that they are isomorphic. For every n at least 3, L(Fn) equals L(Fn+1). So L(F3) equals L(F5), and stretching both by √2 gives L(F2) equals L(F3). By the dichotomy, all free group factors, up to L(F∞), are one, and stretching by any amount gives it back. The free entropy dimensions, built partly to tell these factors apart, are not invariants: each takes every integer value from 2 up on generators of the same algebra.

What it doesn’t claim

That the groups, or their reduced C*-algebras, are isomorphic; or an explicit formula for the isomorphism, which arises as a limit.

How the proof goes

  1. Absorbing an extra generator. Inside L(Fn+1), the proof deforms the first n generators by a trace-preserving flow, so they stay exactly free, while one very long word in them moves far enough to capture the last generator. The key is spectral: a sum of free prefix words puts no spectral mass exactly at zero.
  2. Small steps, a limit, and very long words. Each step moves the generators only slightly, and the errors shrink fast enough that the limit is an isomorphism. The tools are a trace-preserving flow, a Fox calculus cocycle from the 1950s, and Catalan counting, with no free entropy and no random matrices.

2:08How the proof goes

The idea is to absorb an extra generator. Inside L(F_{n+1}), the proof deforms the first n generators by a trace-preserving flow, so they stay exactly free, while one very long word in them moves far enough to capture the last generator. The key is spectral: a sum of free prefix words puts no spectral mass exactly at zero. Our computer, using random matrices as stand-ins, compares: with free prefixes, the mass near zero settles at about one fifth, the Marchenko–Pastur value, while with commuting prefixes it climbs past 90%. Each step moves the generators only slightly, and the errors shrink fast enough that the limit is an isomorphism. The words involved are astronomically long: by our reading of the recipe, accuracy 0.1 needs words about 10¹⁸ letters long. The tools are a trace-preserving flow, a Fox calculus cocycle from the 1950s, and Catalan counting, with no free entropy and no random matrices.

3:12Where it stands

OSVerify has not reviewed it, and no errors have been reported. If it holds, then in the world of von Neumann algebras, two free generators and three are the same.
