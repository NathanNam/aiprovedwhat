Exactly n + 2?

An AI preprint claims the Hovey–Strickland and Chai conjectures, and a public question about where the proof came from

The claim, in plain words

Chromatic homotopy theory studies the stable homotopy category one height at a time. At a prime p and a height n, the K(n)-local category has a natural family of dualizable objects. In their 1999 memoir, Hovey and Strickland found n + 2 thick tensor ideals among them, families closed under the natural operations, and conjectured that there are no others; equivalently, its Balmer spectrum would be a chain of n + 1 points. In 2020, Barthel, Heard and Naumann showed this would follow from a statement about the Lubin–Tate ring, which parametrizes the deformations of a formal group of height n: Chai’s hope that, even for open subgroups of the Morava stabilizer group, the only stable prime ideals are zero and the chain of ideals generated by p, u₁, …. They proved heights 1 and 2, and wrote that higher heights would need substantially new ideas.

Result 311 is one OpenAI paper, 34 pages, dated September 24, 2026, and made public October 6. It claims Chai’s statement at every prime and every positive height, for every open subgroup of the stabilizer. With Barthel, Heard and Naumann, this gives the Hovey–Strickland conjecture: exactly n + 2 thick tensor ideals, and a chain of n + 1 points. For Hovey–Strickland, the new cases are heights 3 and up.

What it doesn’t claim

Anything beyond the dualizable objects, ideals that are not radical, or residue fields other than the finite ones.

A question of provenance

The day after the release, Chenglong Ma, a doctoral student in Copenhagen, posted a proof of the same theorem on arXiv, using the Fargues–Fontaine curve, Cartier theory and Chai’s rigidity theorem; Ningyi Li shared another proof, by different methods, as a public PDF. Ma states that the OpenAI paper’s proof strategy and specific choices of notation are identical to a preliminary version of Ma’s paper that was uploaded to ChatGPT on September 8, 2026, sixteen days before the OpenAI paper’s date. That draft is not public, so we cannot check the claim. In the public versions, the overall strategy is closely parallel, down to some lemmas and a few symbols, but the technical machinery differs, and so do the basic names for the main objects. Both Ma and Li describe their own AI use: Ma used ChatGPT to check the height-3 case and then for auditing, and Li used AI to explore strategies and to audit. We found no response from OpenAI.

The paper is unrefereed and unchanged since release, and there is no Lean formalization. In the first 12 days we found no public specialist assessment of the mathematics and no reported error; the discussion has been about provenance, and we found no response from OpenAI.
