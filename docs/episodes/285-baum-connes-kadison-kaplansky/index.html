The bridge breaks, proved at last?

Three AI preprints claim counterexamples to Baum–Connes and Kadison–Kaplansky

The claim, in plain words

Every group has an algebra of operators built from it, the reduced group C*-algebra. If the group has an element of finite order, averaging over that element’s powers gives a projection, an on-off switch in the algebra; in the cyclic group of order 6, projections have traces that are multiples of 1/6. The Kadison–Kaplansky conjecture says that a torsion-free group, with no such elements, has no projections except 0 and 1. It would follow from the Baum–Connes conjecture of 1982, which says that a bridge, the assembly map, from the geometry of a group to the K-theory of its operator algebra, is a perfect match. In 2002, Higson, Lafforgue and Skandalis showed that, given Gromov’s then unpublished monster groups, whose Cayley graphs carry expanders, a version with coefficients fails. The version without coefficients stayed open: on October 1, 2026, Valette wrote that as of September 2026, no counterexample is known.

Result 285 is three OpenAI preprints, 184 pages, dated September 23 and released October 6, 2026. The first claims a finitely generated torsion-free group where assembly is not even rationally injective: a torus class of infinite order becomes zero in the K-theory of the operator algebra. The second claims a finitely generated torsion-free group whose reduced group C*-algebra contains a projection with trace strictly between 0 and 1/2, a counterexample to Kadison–Kaplansky, and with it a failure of surjectivity. The third gives another failure of surjectivity, through a projection with irrational trace, in a group that does have torsion.

What it doesn’t claim

A counterexample to the Novikov conjecture, which the paper says it does not disprove; groups with finitely many defining relations, or explicit ones. The group ring itself still has no such idempotents: the projection lives only in the completed algebra.

How the proof goes

  1. Expanders planted in a group. All three plant expanders in Cayley graphs by graphical small cancellation, following Gromov and Osajda. A spectral gap puts the projection onto an operator’s kernel inside the C*-algebra itself. In the third paper, that projection’s trace is a sum of powers of 1/p with ever longer gaps, an irrational number, while by Lück’s theorem every class coming from geometry through the assembly map has rational trace.
  2. Towers, squeezes and a Hilbert hotel. The torsion-free examples lift towers of expanding graphs to covers over nilpotent groups. For the projection, a free product squeezes a small matrix projection into a one-by-one projection with the same trace. For injectivity, a torus inside a Heisenberg group is detected by cohomology, yet its image in the operator algebra is cancelled by shifting an infinite tower up a floor, twice.
