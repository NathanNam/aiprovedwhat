The claim, in plain words

A C*-algebra is an algebra of operators, closed in norm and under taking adjoints, and its *-representations send adjoints to adjoints. A bounded homomorphism may forget the adjoint, for example a *-representation seen in a skewed coordinate system. For an oblique projection, an idempotent whose range and kernel meet at an angle, the best straightening has condition number the cotangent of half that angle, about 1.7 at 60 degrees and 7.6 at 15. Kadison asked in 1955 whether every bounded homomorphism from a C*-algebra into operators on a Hilbert space can be straightened this way. Haagerup showed in 1983 that this happens exactly when the map’s norms at all matrix levels share one bound, and Kirchberg showed in 1996 that the question is equivalent to a question about derivations. Averaging settles the amenable cases; the hardest open ones were factors without property Γ, like the free group factor. Pisier conjectured in 1997 that some C*-algebra, probably the reduced C*-algebra of a free group on infinitely many generators, has infinite similarity degree.

Result 288 is one OpenAI preprint, 31 pages, dated September 23, 2026. It claims Kadison’s conjecture is true: every bounded unital homomorphism from any unital complex C*-algebra into operators on a Hilbert space becomes a *-homomorphism after conjugation by a bounded invertible operator. The engine is one absolute constant, about 1.2 million by our arithmetic from the paper, bounding matrix-level commutators with any von Neumann algebra by scalar-level ones. A corollary: every von Neumann algebra is hyperreflexive, with constant at most about 2.4 million.

What it doesn’t claim

A value for Pisier’s similarity degree, any bound on how much the conjugation distorts, or anything about Dixmier’s separate problem for groups. The scope is unital complex C*-algebras and unital homomorphisms.

How the proof goes

  1. From similarity to derivations to free words. Kirchberg’s equivalence turns the problem into one about derivations, and the hard part becomes one constant that works for all finite factors at once. There the proof builds a free companion inside an ultrapower, following Popa, and reads off estimates from long words in the free product, using Schur–Weyl duality to reduce the operator to rearrangements of letters.
  2. A norm that will not shrink. The key step is a Hankel matrix test whose norm does not shrink as the corner shrinks. The paper proves it stays above 1/(2π²); our computer finds 0.417 for corners from 1/10 down to 1/10,000. So the corner size cancels, and later steps carry one constant to every algebra.

The numbers in the video

  • Ours: the best condition number for an oblique projection at angle θ is cot(θ/2): 1.732 at 60°, 7.596 at 15°.
  • The paper’s constants, step by step (our arithmetic): 558, 132,293, 396,882 and C ≈ 1,190,648; the Lean commutator proof’s steps: 16, 1,040, 3,122 and 9,368.
  • Ours: the Hankel norm is 0.417 for corners of trace 1/10 down to 1/10,000, against the paper’s lower bound of about 0.05.
