The claim, in plain words

In a C*-algebra, traces measure the size of elements, and Cuntz comparison asks whether one positive element fits inside another. In 6×6 matrices, the normalized trace of higher and higher roots of a rank-2 element climbs to 2/6; it fits inside a rank-3 element but not a rank-1 one. Strict comparison means that whatever is smaller under every trace fits inside. The Jiang–Su algebra 𝒵 is infinite-dimensional and has no projections except 0 and 1, yet K-theory and traces see it exactly as they see the complex numbers; an algebra unchanged when tensored with it is Z-stable. Nuclear dimension is one less than the number of colours a cover needs: two for an interval, three for a brick-wall cover of a square.

For simple separable nuclear algebras, matrix algebras aside, Toms and Winter conjectured (first written down in 2009) that finite nuclear dimension, Z-stability and strict comparison coincide. By 2021 every direction was proved except strict comparison implies Z-stability, which a recent problem list called “the final open implication.” Result 291 is four OpenAI preprints, 180 pages, dated September 23 and October 5, 2026, and published October 6. The main paper claims that direction for all separable simple nuclear non-elementary algebras, nonunital ones included. Another offers a second proof of the unital case through uniform property Γ, a third claims Robert and Tikuisis’s conjecture on nuclear dimension, and the fourth claims the unital stably finite case of Szabó’s conjecture for amenable group actions, with no restriction on traces.

The same week, and what it doesn’t claim

On October 5 Hannes Thiel posted a proof that strict comparison gives pureness, and on October 7 Evington, Hua, Schafhauser, Seth, Tikuisis and White posted a proof that pureness gives Z-stability, together completing the conjecture. Both human papers credit AI with early help on a key lemma. The release does not claim anything about the UCT, so classification is not completed, or Z-stability beyond nuclear algebras.

How the proof goes

  1. Building blocks. The main paper turns comparison into divisibility, and divisibility into central copies of dimension-drop algebras, the building blocks of 𝒵. With coprime sizes 2 and 3, such a block has only the projections 0 and 1, and K0 = ℤ, K1 = 0.
  2. Splitting every trace at once. The uniform Γ paper uses a central limit theorem: sums of many centered operator coins, cut into equal slices of a bell curve. The equivariant paper does the same along group orbits, with a unitary spread evenly round the circle, cut into arcs.

The numbers in the video

  • Ours: in 6×6 matrices, the normalized trace of a1/k for a rank-2 element reaches 0.3333 at k = 4,096.
  • Ours: with 4,096 coins under a biased trace, centered coins give slices of 0.337, 0.327 and 0.337; uncentered coins give 0, 0 and 1.

2:32How the proof goes

The main paper turns comparison into divisibility, and divisibility into central copies of dimension-drop algebras, the building blocks of the Jiang–Su algebra. Our computer checks such a block: with coprime sizes 2 and 3, its only projections are 0 and 1, and its K-theory matches the complex numbers’. The uniform Gamma paper splits every trace evenly at once with a central limit theorem: sums of many centered operator coins, cut into equal slices of a bell curve. Our computer finds thirds of about 0.33 even under a biased trace, while uncentered coins pile everything into one slice. The equivariant paper does the same along group orbits, with a unitary spread evenly round the circle, cut into arcs.
