Percolation on the square grid?

Six preprints claim SLE and CLE limits for random-cluster interfaces, and Cardy’s formula for square-lattice bond percolation

The claim, in plain words

Keep each edge of the square grid with some probability, and reweight every configuration by q to the power of the number of clusters: the random-cluster model. q = 1 is ordinary bond percolation, q = 2 the cluster picture of the Ising magnet, and q = 3 and 4 Potts models. At the critical point, wire one side of a domain and leave the other free, and a single random curve separates the wired cluster from the rest.

Schramm showed that a limit which is conformally invariant and Markov must be an SLE curve, and Rohde and Schramm predicted κ = 4π/arccos(−√q/2) for q below four: 6 for percolation, 16/3 for Ising. It was proved at q = 2 and in the spanning-tree limit. Smirnov proved Cardy’s crossing formula in 2001, but only for sites on the triangular lattice; bonds on the square grid stayed open.

Result 223 bundles six OpenAI preprints, 602 pages, dated September 23 and October 5, 2026. The main paper claims, for every q from 1 to 4, that these interfaces converge to SLEκ in every bounded Jordan domain, and that all nested loops in the plane converge to the conformal loop ensemble; at q = 1 it states Cardy’s formula for bond percolation on the square grid, every edge open with probability one half. A September version of the same paper covers q from 1 up to 4; another paper covers q between 0 and 1, in smooth domains only. Three more treat Ising with weakly random bonds, Ising slightly off the critical point, and, in the unit square, the number of steps along the curve, which, rescaled, converges to its natural length.

What it doesn’t claim

Anything for q above 4, uniformity in q, loop ensembles below one, or rates of convergence. At q = 4 the proof switches to level lines of the Gaussian free field and rests on a March 2026 preprint by Duminil-Copin, Kozlowski, Lammers and Manolescu.

Bond percolation has a history

Tsai, Yam and Zhou posted a preliminary claim in 2011, and Zhou again in 2024, under extra conditions on the domain’s boundary. The September version credits both as overlapping claims; the October version drops that paragraph. Yu Zhang has a preprint claiming the opposite, that Cardy’s formula fails on the square lattice; no version cites it.

How the proof goes

  1. An observable. Turn clusters into loops, each weighted by √q, and build a complex observable with four boundary changes whose boundary values can be computed exactly.
  2. Holomorphicity. The central difficulty, for q ≠ 2, is proving that its limit is holomorphic; as Riva and Cardy stressed, local identities do not supply the regularity this needs. The papers use six-vertex transfer operators, a spectral factorization of angular modes, and an estimate across a wall between two reflected copies of the field.
  3. Curves and loops. Squeeze any domain between easier and harder polygons on the same lattice (at q = 1 this is Cardy’s formula); the crossing probability is a martingale under exploration, and two tests identify the driving function as √κ times Brownian motion. Loops follow by peeling the plane with a tree of explorations.

The numbers in the video

  • Ours: in a 3-by-3 box at the critical point, all edges open and all closed are equally likely for every q. Interface lengths in boxes up to 512 grow with exponents 1.753, 1.653 and 1.599 for q = 1, 2, 3, near 7/4, 5/3 and 8/5; q = 4 has not settled.
  • Ours: bond-percolation crossings of a 2-by-1 rectangle the long way, 0.172 ± 0.010, against Cardy’s 0.17565. Checks like these go back decades and prove nothing.

2:58How the proof goes

The proof turns clusters into loops, each loop weighted by √q, and builds a complex observable with four boundary changes, whose boundary values can be computed exactly. The central difficulty, for q other than two, is proving that its limit is holomorphic; as Riva and Cardy stressed, local identities do not supply the regularity this needs. The papers attack it with six-vertex transfer operators, a spectral factorization of angular modes, and an estimate across a wall between two reflected copies of the field. With that, Cardy’s formula comes out by squeezing any domain between easier and harder polygons on the same lattice; at q equal to one it is a special case of the general four-change formula. The formula survives exploration, so the crossing probability is a martingale, and two tests identify the driving function as √κ times Brownian motion. The loops follow by peeling the plane with a tree of explorations. As an illustration, our computer sampled critical interfaces in boxes up to 512 wide. Their length grows like the box size to the power 1.75, 1.65, and 1.6 for q equal to one, two and three, close to the SLE dimensions 7/4, 5/3 and 8/5; at q equal to four it has not settled. Bond-percolation crossings of a 2-by-1 rectangle, the long way, came out at 0.172, near Cardy’s 0.176. Checks like these go back decades, and prove nothing.

4:36Where it stands

All six preprints are unrefereed, and none has changed since release. None is formalized in Lean; an unlinked folder proves one finite-graph theorem from the below-one paper. None is among those OSVerify reviewed, and we found no expert reaction. If they hold, the square grid joins the triangular one: the same random curves, and for percolation the same crossing formula, with the Rohde and Schramm prediction settled for q from one up to four, extended to four itself, and below one only in smooth domains.
