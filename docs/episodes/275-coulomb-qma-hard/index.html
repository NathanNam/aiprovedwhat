A hard chemistry problem, proved at last?

The claim, in plain words

Chemistry’s central calculation: given fixed atomic nuclei, find the lowest energy of the electrons around them. For hydrogen it is exactly −1/2 hartree, the atomic unit of energy. For many electrons this is expected to be hard. QMA, the quantum version of NP that Kitaev studied, captures such problems, and lattice and spin versions were proved QMA-hard years ago. In 2021, O’Gorman, Irani, Whitfield and Fefferman proved hardness when the electrons are restricted to a given basis of orbitals, and asked whether it stays hard in the full continuum, with only positively charged nuclei. The catch: restricting electrons to a basis can only raise the energy, so a hard problem in a basis says nothing about whether some exotic continuum state sneaks lower.

Result 275 is two OpenAI preprints, 69 pages, both dated September 24, 2026. They claim that deciding whether this lowest energy is below one threshold or above another is QMA-hard, over all electron states in three dimensions with fixed nuclei, with thresholds at least one hartree apart. One paper uses nuclei with large charges written in binary; the other uses only unit charges, like protons, at rational positions.

What it doesn’t claim

That the problem lies in QMA, so not that it is QMA-complete; anything about real molecules or practical chemistry; or other versions of the question, with a bounded basis error. The hard instances are monsters: in the binary construction a 3×3 grid of spins becomes 66 nuclei and 33 electrons, but each charge has thousands of digits; in the unit construction even toy settings of its formulas give about 1021 protons for only a few hundred electrons, and a one-hartree gap is tiny beside their total energies.

How the proofs go

  1. A hard magnet made of electrons. The proof starts from a quantum magnet known to be hard and builds it out of electrons. Each spin is an electron in its own well, and the magnetic coupling comes from an electron briefly hopping to a neighbour and back, Anderson’s superexchange; in the binary paper’s formula, hydrogen’s on-site repulsion is exactly 5/8 hartree. Signed couplings are made positive with mediator spins.
  2. The whole continuum. The last step is the new one: a lower bound over every continuum state, not only those in a basis. The unit paper also designs smooth wells, each with in-plane energy exactly −1/2, and chops a positive charge cloud into single protons.

The numbers in the video

  • Ours: hydrogen at −0.4999999 hartree; the splitting between the two lowest levels of H2+ shrinks exponentially with proton distance.
  • Ours: the mediator gadget’s error shrinks like 1/√Δ, about nine times inside the paper’s bound.
  • Ours: the sizes of the constructions, from 66 nuclei with charges of thousands of digits to about 1021 unit nuclei at toy settings.
