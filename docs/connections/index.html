    Across labs

    Every link we added between results from different labs, or between OpenAI’s catalogue and an OpenAI result outside it: 18. Our own reading of the papers, not the labs’ data; on the map they are the dashed green lines that leave an arc.

    • S01 (OpenAI, outside its catalogue) same equations 376 (OpenAI’s catalogue). Both are about the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations with a smooth external force: S01 claims finite-time blowup, 376 universal computation.
    • 018 (OpenAI’s catalogue) relies on AX01 (Axiom Math). Both 018 papers say they use the classification of finite simple groups: number fields, “The argument uses the classification of finite simple groups” (build/sections/finite-groups.tex, line 4); function fields, “The proof uses the classification of finite simple groups” (build/paper/A-finite-groups.tex, line 12).
    • 203 (OpenAI’s catalogue) relies on AX01 (Axiom Math). The Donovan paper over algebraic closures of prime fields says “We use the classification of finite simple groups” (build/sections/03_families.tex, line 903). Its Witt-vector companion does not mention it.
    • 206 (OpenAI’s catalogue) relies on AX01 (Axiom Math). Both 206 papers say “We use the classification of finite simple groups” (Finite congruence lattices, build/sections/affine.tex, line 29; Finite lattice representation problem, build/sections/introduction.tex, line 240).
    • 310 (OpenAI’s catalogue) relies on AX01 (Axiom Math). Only through cited reductions: the paper does not name CFSG, but builds on the component reductions of Aschbacher–Smith and Piterman–Smith (build/sections/01-introduction.tex, lines 59–62), which rest on the classification.
    • PSI04 (PSI) cites 272 (OpenAI’s catalogue). “While this manuscript was in preparation, OpenAI released a counterexample to the PPT-squared conjecture that holds in dimension d = 10” (ppt_composition_prl_draft.tex, line 409, citing OpenAI’s result 272).
    • AX02 (Axiom Math) builds on 003 (OpenAI’s catalogue). AX02 cites both of OpenAI’s zero-free preprints: “OpenAI has released two proofs of nonvanishing in a fixed half-plane: Re s > 7/8 … and Re s > 11/12” (DedekindZeta.tex, line 104, Hexagon 2610.00200 v2 source). For the rational numbers its 31/32 is weaker than 003’s 7/8, so it is not an independent check of 003.
    • AX02 (Axiom Math) builds on 029 (OpenAI’s catalogue). AX02 follows 029’s strategy: “Following [Sections 4–8] of OpenAI’s primitive-roots preprint, we reduce nonvanishing of ζ_F to a bound for a smoothed reciprocal” (DedekindZeta.tex, line 191; also line 63).
    • AN03 (Anthropic) cites 378 (OpenAI’s catalogue). Both claim a deterministic algorithm that counts the satisfying assignments of a k-CNF in time 2^O(n log log n/√log n) for fixed k, refuting the Exponential Time Hypothesis; released the same day. Anthropic’s paper: “Independently and concurrently, an internal model at OpenAI claims to have proven a similar result [Ope26]” (paper p. 2, Provenance). OpenAI’s paper does not mention Anthropic.
    • AX10 (Axiom Math) builds on 056 (OpenAI’s catalogue). The README: “The four-dimensional theorem follows OpenAI, Termination for projective log canonical fourfolds with rational boundary (2026)” (README.md, line 5, at commit 1f9ade3); its main theorem MMP.Fourfold.Program.IsMaximal.isNefOnCurves_or_isMoriFibreSpace matches the paper’s Theorem 1.2 by our reading. The four Kähler papers of 056 are not covered.
    • AN06 (Anthropic) implies 003 (OpenAI’s catalogue). Our reading: the companion statement DirichletLNonvanishingRightOfHalf (Statements.lean, lines 6–8) gives L(χ, s) ≠ 0 for Re s > 1/2 for every Dirichlet character, which implies 003’s zero-free half-plane Re s > 7/8 for zeta and every Dirichlet L-function. The note also praises OpenAI’s “Siegel argument”, which we take to be 003’s Landau–Siegel paper (our inference). Independent checkers confirmed on November 21, 2026 that Lean accepts AN06’s proof with only the standard axioms (Sutherland’s log; Lean community statement); there is no paper or peer review yet.
    • AN08 (Anthropic) cites 108 (OpenAI’s catalogue). AN08’s paper cites OpenAI’s cubic lower bound for the border determinantal complexity of the permanent ([OAI26], paper p. 3). Its approximate theorem would imply, by its Remark N.6 (the one numbered statement not formalized in Lean), that the permanent is not approximated by determinants of polynomial size, far beyond a cubic bound, if it holds.
    • 379 (OpenAI’s catalogue) cites AN03 (Anthropic). The Euclidean paper says “The crossing construction originates in work of Anthropic” (Hexagon 2610.00577, Appendix B) and builds on its crossing decompositions; the Hamiltonian-cycle paper cites it, with OpenAI’s 378, as a precedent: “tensor-network algorithms for satisfiability”. (The November 23 paper adapted its grid method.) Anthropic makes Claude, which helps make this series.
    • GO02 (Google) related by topic 142 (OpenAI’s catalogue). Both claim deterministic algorithms that avoid randomness and GRH: GO02 for integer factoring and quadratic nonresidues, 142 for factoring polynomials over prime fields (which would also give deterministic nonresidues by repeated square roots; our remark). Neither cites the other.
    • GO02 (Google) related by topic 003 (OpenAI’s catalogue). 003’s Corollary 1.2 claims a least quadratic nonresidue below C (log p)^A, which would far exceed GO02’s L[1/2] nonresidue bound; GO02 does not cite it (our reading). Both unreviewed.
    • GO04 (Google) related by topic 112 (OpenAI’s catalogue). Both claim to cross a square-root barrier in complexity theory, in different problems: GO04 the exponent 1/2 of the rank for deterministic communication (log-rank conjecture), 112 the 2^√n threshold for explicit depth-three circuit lower bounds. No OpenAI catalogue result concerns log-rank or communication complexity (our search).
    • GO06 (Google) related by topic 136 (OpenAI’s catalogue). Two sides of approximate equilibria: GO06 claims a polynomial-time algorithm for 1/5-approximate Nash equilibria in bimatrix games; 136 (OpenAI) claims a quasilinear PCP theorem for PPAD, whose corollaries are hardness results for approximate equilibria. No OpenAI catalogue result gives an approximate-Nash algorithm (our search).
    • S02 (OpenAI, outside its catalogue) related by topic 260 (OpenAI’s catalogue). Black hole horizons and area: 260 (OpenAI) claims spacetime Penrose inequalities bounding the area of trapped surfaces by the mass in classical gravity; S02 derives the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy of marginally trapped surfaces in a proposed quantum theory.

    Anthropic makes Claude, which helps make this series.
