How to read the record

Every claim gets the same four steps. A filled gold box means a step has happened; an empty box means it hasn’t yet.

Posted as a preprint
The proof is public
Under expert review
A named independent group has reviewed it, or is said to be checking it
Peer reviewed
Refereed by mathematicians
Checked in Lean
Verified step by step by computer

Posted as a preprint means the lab has released a written proof anyone can read. That’s where a claim starts, not where it ends. A claim announced before its paper is public reads Announced, paper not public instead. Under expert review is filled when a named independent group has published a review of the proof (so far OSVerify (a Stanford verification project) for OpenAI’s catalogue, and Kevin Buzzard’s outside check of AN01’s Lean code; a review is not a certification), shown as a blue box when a lab names a group that will review it (we quote who said so), and left empty otherwise. Peer reviewed means independent mathematicians have refereed it, usually for a journal. Checked in Lean means the main result has been verified by Lean, a computer program that accepts a proof only when every step is justified.

When a published Lean proof (OpenAI’s or another lab’s) covers only part of a claim, a half-filled box marks it, on episode pages and in lists, and the record says what is covered.

If a claim is withdrawn, its box turns red and the episode stays up, clearly marked. Mistakes are part of the story.
